# TikTok's For You recommendation system, and how it maps to TNL Labs' ranking code

*Research notes, 8 October 2026. Labels used throughout: **[OFFICIAL]** = TikTok/ByteDance's own statements or papers; **[LEAKED]** = internal documents reported by press; **[REPORTED]** = journalist investigations or audits; **[SECONDARY]** = marketer/creator lore, not confirmed by a primary source; **[SUGGESTION]** = my own recommendation for TNL, not sourced. Repo facts come from a read-only read of `src/server-10-rank.js` and `src/db.js`. Instagram points already covered in `reports/Instagram features and backend.md` (impression beacon, beta-prior smoothing per lab, Instagram's staged expansion and Trial Reels, Not interested / Your Algorithm, `user_lab_affinity`, in-feed surveys) are referenced, not repeated.*

## 1. What TikTok officially says the For You feed uses and avoids

### Takeaway
TikTok's public account (2020, unchanged in substance through 2025) is a per-user ranker over three signal groups: interactions (strong), video information (medium) and device/account settings (weak). Follower count and past hits are explicitly *not* direct factors. Hard diversity rules come first: no two in a row from the same creator or sound, nothing already seen, and dispersal of repetitive themes. User controls followed in stages: Not interested, then "Why this video" (2022), Refresh (2023), and Manage Topics with Smart Keyword Filters (2025).

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL, historical, 18 June 2020]** "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou" lists three signal groups. (1) **User interactions:** videos liked or shared, accounts followed, comments posted, content created. (2) **Video information:** captions, sounds, hashtags. (3) **Device and account settings:** language, country, device type, given "lower weight… since users don't actively express these as preferences." — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL]** On strength of signal, the same post says strong signals outweigh weak ones, and gives as its example that **finishing a longer video is a strong signal**, while viewer and creator being in the same country is a weak one. Each user's feed is unique and ranked by likelihood of interest. — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL]** "Neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors." TikTok concedes popular accounts still get more views indirectly, through their larger audiences. — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL]** **Cold start for new users:** they can pick interest categories (pets, travel). Anyone who skips gets a generalised feed of popular videos, and their first likes, comments and replays start the personalisation. — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL]** **Diversity rules (2020):** the feed "generally won't see two videos in a row made with the same sound or by the same creator". It doesn't recommend duplicates, content already seen, or spam. It deliberately mixes in videos that "may not appear to be relevant to your expressed interests or have amassed a large number of likes", both to help users discover and to let the system "get a better sense of what's popular among a wider range of audiences". The post names the filter-bubble risk directly. — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL]** **Eligibility:** newly uploaded videos, videos under review, and spam built to inflate traffic "may be ineligible" for recommendation. — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL]** **Negative feedback:** long-press, then "Not interested". Users can also hide a creator or a sound, or report a video. The ranker starts "from interests you express as a new user and adjust[s] for things you indicate you're not interested in." — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); [TikTok Newsroom, "Why this video", Dec 2022](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/why-this-tiktok?lang=en-GB)
- **[OFFICIAL, 20 Dec 2022]** The "Why this video?" panel gives four kinds of reason: your interactions (watch, like, share, comment, **search**); accounts you follow or that are suggested to you; **content posted recently in your region**; and **content popular in your region**. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/why-this-tiktok?lang=en-GB)
- **[OFFICIAL, 16 Mar 2023]** **Refresh** lets a user see the For You feed "as if they just signed up". Hidden creators, keyword filters and follows survive the reset. The same post restates "won't see two videos in a row from the same creator or using the same sound" and "we try to avoid showing people something they've seen before". **Dispersal:** when several videos on themes that are fine once but harmful in a run (sadness, extreme dieting or exercise, sexually suggestive content) land in the eligible set, some are swapped for other topics. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-ie/safeguarding-recommendations-update); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/03/16/tiktok-rolling-out-for-you-feed-reset-button/)
- **[OFFICIAL, 3 June 2025]** **Manage Topics** puts a frequency slider on 10+ categories ("Creative Arts, Travel, Nature and Sports"). It "won't eliminate any topics entirely" but changes how often they come up. It was piloted in the US the year before. **Smart Keyword Filters** expand a blocked word to synonyms ("remodeling" also catches "renovation"); coverage reports the keyword limit rose from 100 to 200. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-discover-new-content-and-creators-you-love); [etcentric](https://www.etcentric.org/?p=193503)
- **[REPORTED, current-status context]** The TikTok USDS Joint Venture formed in January 2026 under a 25 Sept 2025 executive order. It is to "retrain, test, and update the content recommendation algorithm on U.S. user data", hosted and audited by Oracle. One report says the algorithm is "leased" from ByteDance. — [The Register, Jan 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/23/jv_announces_tiktok_us_acquisition/); [Domino Theory](https://dominotheory.com/tiktok-finally-under-new-ownership-algorithm-to-be-leased-from-china/)

### Inferences
- TikTok's own list of "what counts" names only interactions. Watch time appears only as an example ("finishing a longer video"), but the leaks and audits in Section 2 make it central. Official copy undersells it.
- Several of the official rules are cheap and deterministic, and TNL can copy them almost one for one: no two in a row from the same creator, never re-show seen items, deliberate off-interest slots, and dispersal of repeated topics. TNL already has a soft version of the first (`repeatDamp` 0.55 per extra piece by the same author on the page). It has none of the others, because it records no "seen" beyond likes and comments.
- "Not a direct factor: follower count" is a design stance, and it maps onto TNL's rule that rep comes only from what others do with your work. TNL's `followBoost` 1.3 is a viewer-side follow signal, which is consistent with TikTok, where following personalises your own feed but does not lift the creator globally.
- TikTok's Manage Topics categories line up with TNL's labs (`posts.channel`), so an editable lab affinity is the TikTok control as well as the Instagram one.

### Gaps
- I found no official TikTok statement after 2023 listing new signal types such as search history or off-app data. The 2022 "Why this video" post adds search and region-recency only.
- I could not confirm whether the Smart Keyword Filter "select/deselect expansions" option has shipped, or the exact current keyword limit.
- No public detail yet on what US retraining changed in ranking behaviour (as of Oct 2026).
- The Transparency Center / "Algorithm & Transparency" pages were not fetched directly. The newsroom posts above are the official statements I checked.

## 2. Leaked and reported internals: the value formula, watch time, heating, test pools

### Takeaway
The best-sourced internal view is the 2021 "TikTok Algo 101" leak. It shows a weighted sum of predicted actions (like, comment, play, expected playtime), optimised for retention and time spent, with penalties for same-day category repeats and for engagement bait. The WSJ's bot study found that watch time and rewatches dominated how quickly the system learned. Manual "heating" exists and is small but non-trivial (Forbes 2023). The widely described "traffic pool" test-then-expand model rests on very weak sourcing: only "test on a small audience first" holds up, and the specific thresholds are folklore.

### Cited Findings
- **[LEAKED, historical, NYT 5 Dec 2021]** The Beijing engineering team's "TikTok Algo 101" document gives the "ultimate goal" as adding daily active users, pursued through two metrics: **retention** and **time spent**. Its simplified score is **Plike×Vlike + Pcomment×Vcomment + Eplaytime×Vplaytime + Pplay×Vplay**, where P are model-predicted probabilities, E an estimated value and V assigned weights. The doc itself calls this "highly simplified". TikTok confirmed the document is authentic. — [The Batch / DeepLearning.AI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/what-makes-tiktok-tick); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/leaked-tiktok-doc-reveals-its-obvious-secret-to-an-addi-1848166901)
- **[LEAKED]** The same document describes boosting producers the user has watched before, **penalising categories the user already watched earlier that day** (against boredom), and **penalising videos that explicitly ask for likes**. It ties "video quality" partly to creators' upload rate and monetisation. — [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/what-makes-tiktok-tick)
- **[REPORTED, historical, WSJ July 2021]** The WSJ ran 100+ bot accounts with hidden interests. Bots lingered on or rewatched matching videos and swiped past the rest, and **never entered an interest**. TikTok worked out most bots' interests in **under two hours**. The heaviest signals were **watch time, repeat views and pauses**. One "sadness" bot reached a feed that was 93% depressive content after 224 videos (about 33 min). TikTok said bots are unrepresentative. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-wall-street-journal-guillaume-chaslot/); [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/what-makes-tiktok-tick); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2021/07/28/how-tiktoks-algorithm-works/)
- **[REPORTED, Forbes Jan 2023]** "Heating" is defined in the internal "MINT Heating Playbook" as "boosting videos into the For You feed through operation intervention to achieve a certain number of video views". Heated videos made up **about 1–2% of daily total video views**. Stated aims were attracting influencers and brands, diversifying content and pushing important information. Some employees abused it for their own accounts. TikTok confirmed heating exists and said about **0.002%** of US For You videos are heated. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/01/23/tiktok-admits-its-own-employees-can-decide-what-videos-go-viral/); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2023/01/23/tiktok-for-you-page-heating-recommend-videos-algorithm/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/tiktok-has-a-secret-heating-cheating-button-391983)
- **[SECONDARY]** The "traffic pool" model: a new video goes to a small batch, then to larger pools if early completion and engagement beat a bar. The most careful secondary source says no TikTok primary source names fixed thresholds (e.g. "500 views"). Only the small-audience-first behaviour is reasonably established. Figures like "200–500 people" are unverified creator lore. — [blck alpaca knowledge base](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-page); [ShortSync guide](https://shortsync.app/guides/tiktok-algorithm-explained)
- **[SECONDARY]** Douyin marketing guides describe tag-matching between users and creators, then a decision on whether to push to a wider pool, with completion rate as a key metric. These are marketer sources with unverified benchmarks. — [KAWO](https://kawo.com/en/blog/five-key-metrics-that-help-brands-get-on-douyins-algorithm-and-gain-more-traffic)
- **[OFFICIAL, TikTok 2020, indirect support]** TikTok's statement that just-uploaded videos "may be ineligible" fits a staged rollout, but TikTok does not describe pools. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[OFFICIAL, comparable platforms]** Staged seed-audience expansion is officially described by YouTube (Shorts "seed audience", see Section 5) and by Instagram (April 2024; covered in the Instagram report). That makes the *pattern* industry-standard even though TikTok's own numbers are unknown. — [TechCrunch, YouTube Shorts 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)

### Inferences
- The Algo-101 shape (Σ P(action)·V(action), plus an expected-time term) is the same "value model" Instagram uses. What sets TikTok apart is the **Eplaytime** and **Pplay** terms: consumption *before* any explicit action. TNL's score is entirely explicit actions (the numerator of Σ), so it misses the signal that, per WSJ and the 2025 audit replication, carries the most information per impression.
- "Penalise categories already watched earlier today" is a per-viewer, per-day topic damp. TNL can implement it over labs with no ML.
- Heating is a reminder that staff boosts exist even at TikTok, at about 1–2% of views. If TNL ever adds an "editor's pick", it should be logged and capped, and arguably labelled, in keeping with TNL's rule that rep is never self-awarded.

### Gaps
- The full NYT article and the original WSJ article/video were not fetched directly (paywalled). Details come from reputable secondary coverage citing them.
- No primary source gives TikTok's test-pool sizes, promotion thresholds, or the V weights in the Algo-101 formula.
- I found no reliable primary source quantifying completion-rate or rewatch weights. The 2020 official post's "finishing a longer video is a strong signal" is the only first-party statement.

## 3. ByteDance research: Monolith, real-time training and long-sequence user modelling

### Takeaway
ByteDance's distinctive edge, as published, is **freshness of the model**, not a secret formula. Monolith (2022) trains online from a Kafka/Flink stream of joined actions. It pushes sparse embedding updates to serving about **every minute**, and showed that shorter sync intervals and online training beat batch. Recent ByteDance papers (LONGER 2025, Douyin 10k-sequence 2025, HyFormer 2026) scale models of a user's behaviour sequence to thousands of past items. For TNL, "real time" translates to "update scores and the viewer's interest vector on every event, not nightly".

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL, ByteDance paper, Sept 2022]** *Monolith: Real Time Recommendation System With Collisionless Embedding Table*, arXiv 2209.07663, ORSUM @ RecSys 2022. It argues TensorFlow/PyTorch handle sparse, dynamic features badly and separate batch training from serving, which "prevents the model from interacting with customer feedback in realtime". Its contributions are a collisionless embedding table with **expirable embeddings and frequency filtering**, and a fault-tolerant online-training architecture. It says "system reliability could be traded-off for real-time learning", and that Monolith "has successfully landed in the BytePlus Recommend product". — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07663)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Monolith internals: a **cuckoo hash table** (two sub-tables; worst-case O(1) lookups); IDs **expire after a tunable period of inactivity**; IDs must reach a tunable occurrence count before getting an embedding. On MovieLens, hashing would have collided 7.73% of user IDs and 2.86% of movie IDs, which is the argument for being collisionless. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.07663v2)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Streaming pipeline: user actions and features go into Kafka. A Flink job **joins each action to the features logged at request time** by a unique request key, keeping late arrivals in on-disk KV. Joined examples feed online training directly. **Negative sampling** is used, with **log-odds correction at serving** to keep estimates unbiased. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.07663v2)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Sync: **sparse parameters sync about every minute** (only the IDs touched); dense parameters sync **daily**. On Criteo, average AUC for online vs batch was 79.66 vs 79.42 at a 5h sync interval, 79.78 vs 79.44 at 1h, and 79.80 vs 79.43 at 30 min, so fresher is better. A live A/B on an ads model reported online training beating batch by **14.0–18.1% (relative "AUC improvement" as reported) across seven days**. Parameter-server snapshots were taken **daily** with "nearly no" quality loss. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.07663v2)
- **[OFFICIAL, ByteDance, May 2025]** *LONGER: Scaling Up Long Sequence Modeling in Industrial Recommenders* is a Transformer for ultra-long behaviour sequences using global tokens, token merge and hybrid attention, with KV-cache serving. It beat baselines offline and in online A/B tests in ads and e-commerce, and is deployed in "more than 10 influential scenarios at ByteDance". — [arXiv 2505.04421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04421v1)
- **[OFFICIAL, ByteDance/Douyin, Nov 2025]** *Make It Long, Keep It Fast: End-to-End 10k-Sequence Modeling* replaces history self-attention with **target-to-history cross-attention**, so cost is linear in history length. It adds request-level batching (one user encoding shared across all candidates) and trains on short windows but infers on long ones. It is fully deployed on Douyin with "significant improvements on key engagement metrics". — [arXiv 2511.06077](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06077v1)
- **[OFFICIAL, ByteDance, Jan 2026]** HyFormer, a hybrid long-sequence plus feature-interaction transformer for CTR, reported **+0.293% average watch time per user** in Douyin Search online A/B. — [alphaXiv overview of arXiv 2601.12681](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2601.12681)

### Inferences
- What "real time" means at ByteDance: a user's swipe changes the *model's embeddings* within about a minute, and the next request scores against them. Combined with sub-second watch-time signals from a full-screen, one-item-at-a-time UI, the loop closes within one session. That fits the WSJ's "interests learned in under two hours" (Section 2). The UI makes every impression a labelled example: watched to the end, rewatched, or skipped. A grid or scroll feed gives far weaker labels.
- Monolith's ideas that scale down: (a) **join each action to what was shown** (the request key, i.e. an impression id), so every like or skip has a denominator; (b) **expire stale state**, so an interest vector decays; (c) **frequency filtering**, so no personalised weight is learned from one event. TNL needs no embeddings: lab and tag affinities are its "embedding table", and with hundreds to low thousands of members, SQLite updates per event are trivially cheap.
- Long-sequence modelling says the *ordered history* of what a viewer consumed is the main personalisation input. TNL's equivalent is a short, decayed list of labs, tags and creators the viewer recently finished or responded to.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the RankMixer paper (ByteDance scaling of the ranking model) or ByteDance multi-task papers (e.g. MMoE-style multi-objective heads for like/finish/share). They are not cited here because I did not verify them.
- BytePlus Recommend's public product documentation was not fetched. The link to it rests only on the Monolith abstract.

## 4. Interest graph vs social graph, cold start, and what audits measured

### Takeaway
TikTok works without follows because each full-screen impression is a labelled example and the feed is ranked by inferred interest rather than by who you follow. Audits measure roughly **30–50% of the feed as "exploitation" of known interests**, the rest exploration or noise. Personalisation stabilises after about **100 videos**. Whether follows or watch time matter more for personalisation is **disputed between audits**, and audit results go stale quickly.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Users who skip onboarding get popular videos, and personalisation starts from first likes, comments and replays. Videos with few likes are deliberately mixed in. — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[REPORTED, academic audit, WWW '24]** Vombatkere et al., "TikTok and the Art of Personalization", used 347 donated real-user histories (9.2M views). The mean exploit fraction is ≥50% for real users, about 31% for bots and about 20% for a randomised baseline (noise floor), so the authors conclude TikTok exploits interests in **30–50% of the first 1,000 videos**. The exploit fraction is stable after **about 100 videos**. Personalisation correlates most with the **fraction from followed creators** (p≈10⁻¹⁴) and the **fraction liked** (p≈10⁻⁵). **Watch percentage** has a medium effect (84% vs 93%, p=0.03), and the **early-skip rate is not significant**. — [arXiv 2403.12410](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12410v1)
- **[REPORTED, academic replication, 2025]** "Revisiting Algorithmic Audits of TikTok: Poor Reproducibility and Short-term Validity of Findings" found **watching longer was the strongest personalisation signal, stronger than following**, which contradicts Boeker & Urman and Vombatkere et al. It warns that users can drift into niche or extreme content "without any explicit actions, just by watching some videos longer", and that audit findings expire quickly. — [arXiv 2504.18140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18140); [KInIT summary](https://kinit.sk/?p=36519)
- **[REPORTED]** Former YouTube recommender engineer Guillaume Chaslot, quoted in the WSJ coverage, estimated "90–95%" of content seen on TikTok comes from the recommender. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-wall-street-journal-guillaume-chaslot/)

### Inferences
- **Interest graph:** the follow graph is one feature among many. The unit of personalisation is the topic or sound plus the creator, learned from consumption. TNL's labs (`posts.channel`), tags and linked audio (`posts.audio_track_id`) are natural interest dimensions.
- **Cold start for users** at TikTok means onboarding picks plus popular-by-default plus fast exploration. TNL has an advantage: the labs a member joins at signup are an explicit interest declaration.
- **Cold start for posts** means a guaranteed small test audience. On a network of hundreds of members, "small" must be absolute (e.g. 15–30 viewers), not TikTok-scale, and the bar should be relative to the lab median (see recipes).
- **Risk lesson:** the WSJ and replication rabbit-hole findings show that a pure watch-time optimiser narrows fast. TNL should cap any single lab's share per page and keep exploration slots.

### Gaps
- No primary source on TikTok's exact explore/exploit share or exploration policy (bandit type, epsilon). Audits give only observed fractions under their own definitions.
- The Boeker & Urman (2022) audit was not fetched directly. It is mentioned through the 2025 replication.

## 5. Comparison with Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts (not repeating the Instagram report)

### Takeaway
All three platforms converge on the same structure: staged distribution to a seed audience, a weighted multi-action value model, and a consumption-based "real view" signal. The differences are emphasis. TikTok leans hardest on watch time and interest over social graph. YouTube Shorts explicitly measures "viewed vs swiped away" and pushes to a seed audience. Instagram (see the Instagram report) adds sends-per-reach and an original-content preference.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL, YouTube, Aug 2023]** Shorts lead Todd Sherman: a Shorts view requires a "meaningful threshold" of intent to watch, which is unpublished and periodically tweaked to resist gaming. Studio shows creators "**viewed vs. swiped away**". When a Short takes off and stalls, the algorithm has found a **seed audience** and grows or tapers based on its response. Most Shorts traffic comes from the feed. Posting frequency is "not a factor". — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/)
- **[SECONDARY]** From 31 March 2025, Shorts views count on any start or replay, and "Engaged Views" drive monetisation metrics. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[OFFICIAL, Instagram]** Instagram's small-audience-first expansion (April 2024), Trial Reels, and Mosseri's top signals (watch time, likes-per-reach, sends-per-reach) are already documented in `reports/Instagram features and backend.md`. Primary: [TechCrunch 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)

### Inferences
- The one TikTok-specific lesson not already in the Instagram report is the **skip/finish label per impression** (YouTube's "viewed vs swiped away" is the same idea), together with the **per-day topic fatigue penalty** and the **hard no-two-in-a-row rule**.

### Gaps
- I found no official current (2026) YouTube Shorts ranking explainer beyond the 2023 interview.

## 6. Practical recipes for TNL's SQLite ranking (all constants are [SUGGESTION])

### Takeaway
TNL can get most of TikTok's benefit with four additions, all without ML or new dependencies:
1. A **per-impression consumption log** (seen / dwell / listen-through / skip), built on the Instagram report's impression beacon.
2. A **tiered test-audience** state per post that replaces the flat `newCreatorLift`.
3. A **decayed per-viewer lab, tag and creator affinity** updated on every event.
4. A **re-ranking pass** with exploration slots, no-two-in-a-row and per-day topic fatigue.

### Cited Findings
- TNL today: score = (1 + Σ standingWeight(rep_responder) + comment extras + collab lift + new-creator lift (+1 for first 3 works)) / (age_h + 3)^1.35. It has follow ×1.3, seen (liked or commented) ×0.45, own ×0.8 and greedy author damping ×0.55^k. There are no impressions, consumption, negative feedback or interest model. — repo `src/server-10-rank.js` lines 23–104 (read-only). *Note: the brief and the Instagram report say DM sends were shipped (PR #36, `sendExtra` 0.75), but the copy of `server-10-rank.js` on disk at the time of reading has no DM-send term. Check which branch is live.*
- Posts carry `channel` (the lab), `video_url`, `audio_track_id`, `beat_json` and `image_url`, so media type and lab are available for consumption measurement and affinity. — repo `src/db.js` lines 61–73, 496–517 (read-only)
- TikTok's basis for each recipe is cited in Sections 1–4 above: finishing as a strong signal, no repeats, off-interest mixing, Not interested, refresh, topic sliders, the per-day category penalty, and online updates.

### Inferences (concrete recipes, all constants are suggestions)

**R1. Consumption events (the Eplaytime / Pplay analogue).** This extends the Instagram report's `/api/seen` impression beacon with per-media completion.
- Table `views(post_id, viewer_id, day, shown_ms, dwell_ms, play_ms, media_ms, completes, replays, skipped, PRIMARY KEY(post_id, viewer_id, day)) WITHOUT ROWID`. Upsert, keeping the max of `dwell_ms` and `play_ms` and summing `replays`.
- **Listen-through / watch-through:** `lt = min(1, play_ms / media_ms)`. Count a **complete** at `lt ≥ 0.9` (or `play_ms ≥ 60s` for tracks longer than ~3 min, so long tracks aren't penalised). Count a **replay** when the `<audio>/<video>` `ended` event fires and play restarts, or `currentTime` seeks back to under 10% after passing 50%.
- **Skip:** the card was ≥50% visible but `dwell_ms < 1500` and `play_ms < 3000` (or play started and stopped at `lt < 0.15` before 5s). TikTok audits found early skips a weak signal for personalisation ([arXiv 2403.12410](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12410v1)), so treat a skip as a *weak* negative: −0.2 weight on affinity, nothing on global score unless the skip rate is extreme.
- Images (no duration): use dwell ≥ 4s as the "complete" analogue, and opening the post detail as a "play".
- Ignore events from the author, from suspended users, and anything past the first 3 impressions per viewer per post per day. This matches the existing "only what others do" rule.

**R2. Per-post quality as smoothed rates.** Use the Instagram report's beta prior toward the lab median; here it is extended with TikTok's consumption terms. For post p in lab L, over unique non-author viewers with an impression:
- `reach = n_viewers`, `C = completes`, `R = viewers who replayed`, `S = skips`, `E = Σ standingWeight·(1 + 0.5·comment)` (the existing responder sum).
- `cr = (C + k·μ_cr[L]) / (reach + k)`; `er = (E + k·μ_er[L]) / (reach + k)`; `sr = (S + k·μ_sr[L]) / (reach + k)`; with k = 10, and μ the lab median over the last 30 days (fallback: global median, then constants cr 0.35, er 0.08, sr 0.4).
- **Value score (Algo-101 shape):** `V = 1.0·(cr/μ_cr) + 1.5·(er/μ_er) + 0.5·(R/(reach+k))/max(0.02, μ_rr) − 0.5·(sr/μ_sr − 1)`. A post at its lab median scores about 3.0. Keep collab lift additive (+0.3 per accepted collaborator, max 3) so the collab rule is kept without letting it dominate.
- Final: `score = V · decay`, with `decay = 1/(age_h + 3)^1.2` (slightly gentler than the current 1.35, because quality now has a denominator and old-and-good posts are no longer just old-and-exposed).

**R3. Tiered test audience (replaces `newCreatorLift`).** Add a `post_tier(post_id, tier, reach_at_tier, decided_at)` row, updated whenever a view or response arrives (R5).
- Tier 0, **seed**: every new work is guaranteed to reach **N0 = 15 distinct viewers** (or 10% of weekly-active members in its lab, whichever is larger, capped at 40) within 24h. Get there by reserving **2 of every 10 Showroom slots** for tier-0 posts, matched to viewers whose lab affinity for the post's lab is in their top 3. Tier-0 posts go to non-followers first, as in TikTok and Instagram trials, so the test is not inflated by friends.
- Promotion test at `reach ≥ N0`: promote to **tier 1 (N1 = 60)** if `V ≥ median V of the lab's posts at the same tier over 30 days`. Promote to **tier 2 (open)** at reach ≥ N1 if `V ≥ lab 60th percentile`. Otherwise the post stays in the organic pool with its normal score: never buried, just not boosted. Hysteresis: never demote below tier 0's organic score.
- Give new creators' first 3 works N0 = 25. That replaces the flat +1 with exposure, which is what the +1 was trying to buy.
- At TNL's scale, a seed of 15 is noisy. The beta prior (k = 10) keeps decisions conservative. Log every promotion as an event so it can be audited, the way heating should have been.

**R4. Per-viewer interest model over labs, tags and creators, with decay.** This is the same table idea as the Instagram report's `user_lab_affinity`, specified here with TikTok-style event weights and decay.
- `affinity(viewer_id, kind, key, w, updated_at)`, where kind ∈ {lab, tag, creator, sound}.
- Event increments: complete +1.0, replay +1.5, like +1.0, save/pin +2.0, comment +2.0, share/DM send +2.5, follow +3.0 (creator), skip −0.2, **Not interested −4.0** (and hide-creator, which sets the creator weight to −∞), and onboarding lab pick +5.0 as the prior.
- Decay is applied lazily on read and write: `w ← w·0.5^((now − updated_at)/HL)`, with half-life **HL = 14 days** for lab and tag and 30 days for creator. That is Monolith's "expirable embeddings" in miniature.
- Frequency filter, as in Monolith: ignore a key until it has ≥ 2 positive events, so one accidental tap doesn't personalise.
- Use at rank time: `p_lab = softmax(w_lab / τ)` with τ = 3 over the viewer's labs, then a multiplier `m = 0.6 + 0.8·p_lab(post) / max(p_lab)` (range 0.6–1.4) and `·1.25` for a positive creator weight. This replaces nothing; it multiplies R2's score. It keeps the existing `followBoost`, or folds it into the creator weight.
- **Refresh** (TikTok 2023): delete the viewer's affinity rows except hidden creators and filters. **Manage Topics** (TikTok 2025): a per-lab slider stored as a fixed multiplier 0.25–2.0 that the viewer can see.

**R5. Real-time-ish updates (Monolith's lesson at SQLite scale).** Update `post_stats` and `affinity` **synchronously inside the request** that records the like, view batch or skip. Each is a single-row UPSERT, microseconds in SQLite at TNL scale. Recompute tier decisions on the same write. Keep the full rank as an on-read function (as today), with a 30–60s per-viewer memo cache. Optionally run a 5-minute job to recompute the lab medians μ. Batch-only nightly scoring would throw away the within-session loop that made TikTok learn bots' interests in under two hours.

**R6. Exploration share and re-ranking (TikTok's diversity rules).** Run this after scoring, in the existing greedy loop:
- **Slot plan per 10 cards** (signed-in viewer with ≥ 20 affinity events): 6 exploit (top score × affinity), 2 test-audience (R3 tier 0), and **2 explore**: posts from labs outside the viewer's top 3, sampled ∝ score. Viewers with fewer events get 4 / 2 / 4, which is TikTok's popular-plus-diverse cold start. This sits near the audits' observed 30–50% exploit share for heavy users, but TNL's network is small, so explore more.
- **Hard rules:** never two adjacent cards by the same author; never two adjacent from the same linked sound/`audio_track_id`; never re-show a post the viewer had an impression of in the last 7 days unless they replayed it; drop posts marked Not interested, and creators hidden.
- **Per-day topic fatigue** (Algo 101's same-day category penalty): multiply by `0.85^(n_today(lab))`, where n_today is how many of the viewer's impressions today were in that lab. This sits on top of the existing author damping (keep `repeatDamp` 0.55).
- **Dispersal cap:** no lab may take more than 50% of any 20-card page unless the viewer only follows or has only joined that lab.

**R7. Measurement.** A/B by `viewer_id % 2` (already proposed in the Instagram report). TikTok's north stars were retention and time spent ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/what-makes-tiktok-tick)). For a creative community, use [SUGGESTION] **share of new works reaching N0 within 24h**, **median listen-through**, **7-day return rate**, and **unique responders per creator per week (Gini)**. Avoid pure time-spent, given the rabbit-hole evidence.

**Things not to copy.** An optimisation target of raw time spent; unlogged manual boosts; and dropping follower count entirely. TNL is small, so follows are a valid strong signal here, and the Vombatkere audit found follows to be the strongest personalisation correlate anyway.

### Gaps
- No TikTok source gives tier sizes, promotion bars, decay half-lives or exploration share. Every number in R1–R7 is a starting point to tune with TNL's own data.
- Whether the browser's `<audio>/<video>` events are reliable enough on iOS Safari for listen-through (background tabs, autoplay restrictions) was not researched. It needs an e2e step per CLAUDE.md.
- The existing `server-10-rank.js` part is about 7.7KB (per the Instagram report) and should fit R2–R6. The view beacon route and affinity updates would likely need a new `server-NN-*` part to stay under the 24KB limit.
