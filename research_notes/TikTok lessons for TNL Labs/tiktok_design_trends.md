# TikTok design, UX and trend/remix mechanics: lessons for TNL Labs (as of October 2026)

Scope note: this covers TikTok's interface, interaction mechanics, remix/trend culture, and the regulatory debate, with vanilla-JS patterns TNL could use. It does not repeat the Instagram notes in `reports/Instagram lessons for TNL Labs.md`. "Historical" marks items more than about two years old. TNL context comes from the repo README (read-only): the Music lab is a player with an auto-advancing queue; rep is +4 to a sound's owner when "someone builds with a sound you shared"; events have bracket and poll formats, hidden counts while a stage is open (except the poll scoreboard), and Instagram vote links that lead back to the app (`EVFOCUS`).

## 1. Interface design: why TikTok feels the way it does

### Takeaway
The core idea is one item at a time. A full-screen, paged feed asks a single question per screen ("how do you feel about this video?"), and every swipe, loop or rewatch answers it, so the feed learns without asking anything. The brand and type system stay quiet: a dark canvas, a custom legible sans, and a glitch-note logo whose cyan/red split is the only flourish.

### Cited Findings
- (Historical, 2020) Eugene Wei argues the For You feed "is not a scrolling feed. It's paginated, effectively". In an infinite feed the algorithm can't tell "which story your eyes rest on", but with one video on screen, "Everything you do from the moment the video begins playing is signal as to your sentiment towards that video." Looping, sharing, opening the profile and following are positive signals, and "A quick swipe up before a video has completed is like swiping left on Tinder." Next videos load in the background so playback starts quickly. — [Eugene Wei, "Seeing Like an Algorithm" (Sept 2020)](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2020/9/18/seeing-like-an-algorithm)
- Wei frames the single-video layout as asking "how do you feel about this short video and this short video alone?" He says pagination adds friction but gives a cleaner signal, and that "Some forms of friction are good." — [Eugene Wei](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2020/9/18/seeing-like-an-algorithm)
- Per-video watch time works as granular, continuous implicit feedback that costs the user no effort, and short videos mean one session yields many data points (about 20 videos per session, each adding an entry to the user-item matrix). — [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/why-tiktok-made-its-user-so-obsessive-the-ai-algorithm-that-got-you-hooked-7895bb1ab423)
- A 2025 ICWSM paper (Kaplan et al., Northeastern), using controlled accounts, found the FYP responds to both explicit and implicit signals, and that the most effective explicit signal, "Not Interested", is hard to find in the interface. — [ICWSM 2025, "When 'For You' Isn't for You"](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42688)
- Coverage republished in July 2026 reports the algorithm leans heavily on implicit signals such as watch duration, and users often keep seeing content they skipped or marked "not interested". — [Ars Technica via Harvard tagteam](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3382/feed_items/18534689)
- A 2024 study estimated the algorithm personalises 30–50% of recommended videos in a user's first 1,000, with liking and following as the main drivers. — [arXiv 2403.12410](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12410v1)
- TikTok Sans was introduced on 17 May 2023, made with Grilli Type, to optimise "legibility and reading retention". It has "bigger openings and clearer strokes", is larger than the previous Proxima Nova, uses increased line height and a revised letter-spacing formula, and launched in 9 languages. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-sans)
- The zero gained a slash to separate it from "o". Users' reaction was largely negative ("ugly"). — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/tiktok-font); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/05/18/tiktok-is-rolling-out-a-custom-tiktok-sans-font/)
- Type Network later extended TikTok Sans with Contrast Foundry (Maria Doreuli, Krista Radoeva; new width masters), hinting by Aaron Bell and production by Guido Ferreyra. It has weight, slant, optical-size and width axes, covers Latin, Greek and Cyrillic, and was open-sourced on Google Fonts (the case study gives no exact date). — [Type Network case study](https://typenetwork.com/articles/tiktok-sans-open-source-case-study); [PRINT](https://www.printmag.com/design-news/type-network-expands-tiktok-sans-from-brand-typeface-to-open-source/)
- Logo: a stylised eighth note, also read as a lowercase "d" for Douyin, built from three offset layers (cyan #25F4EE shadow, red-pink #FE2C55 shadow, white foreground on black), which gives a chromatic-aberration "glitch". The colours are said to evoke concert stage lighting; the specific "rock concert" origin story appears in only one source and is unverified. The 2018 update made the wordmark one word with custom lettering. — [Shopify](https://shopify.com/blog/tiktok-logo); [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/tiktok-brand-guidelines/); [brandkit.pro (unverified origin claim)](https://brandkit.pro/brand/tiktok)
- (Historical, 2021, then 2023) A seekable progress bar appeared on longer videos from about 2021, and in January 2023 TikTok added scrubbing thumbnails that preview the spot you're dragging to. — [The Star (2021)](https://thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2021/07/09/you-can-now-fast-forward-tiktok-videos); [Tubefilter (2023)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/01/06/tiktok-video-scrubbing-thumbnails-long-form-videos/)

### Inferences
- Lesson for TNL: in places where judgement matters (event voting, the Music lab), a one-at-a-time view gives each piece its full moment and turns "next" into a meaningful act. TNL's events already hide vote counts during a stage, which fits a focus view that judges each piece alone, without social proof.
- The scrubber appearing only on longer videos is chrome that shows up on demand. TNL could show a seek bar only when a track or video is longer than about 30–60 s, and keep short loops clean.
- TikTok's cyan/red glitch belongs to TikTok. TNL should not copy it (its palette rule is white/black/#98FC68, and every colour goes through `src/palette.js`). The transferable idea is having one memorable accent effect and nothing else. TNL's equivalent is the green accent and the `//` lab marker.
- TikTok Sans exists to make captions legible over moving video. TNL uses Helvetica, and the transferable lesson is the treatment rather than the face: generous line height, and a text shadow or scrim on captions over media. Any scrim colour must come from the palette tokens.

### Gaps
- I found no primary TikTok source describing the right-side action rail, the spinning record (sound disc) icon, the scrolling sound ticker, or its dark-UI rationale. These are well-known from using the app but uncited here.
- I found no primary source for "sound-on by default" as a stated design decision, and no source on how TikTok's native app handles prefetch or first frame internally, beyond Wei's note that videos load in the background.

## 2. Interaction mechanics, accessibility, wellbeing tools and the addictive-design debate

### Takeaway
TikTok's mechanics (instant swipe, autoplay, loop, infinite feed) are now named in regulatory findings. The EU Commission's preliminary view in February 2026 is that infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications and the recommender breach the DSA, and that opt-in screen-time tools are not enough. Fourteen US AGs sued in October 2024 over autoplay that can't be turned off and over beauty filters. A small creative app should borrow the focus, not the bottomlessness.

### Cited Findings
- Accessibility, 2021 (historical): auto captions launched on 6 April 2021 (American English and Japanese first). Creators enable and edit them in the editor, and viewers can turn them off from the share panel. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-auto-captions)
- 2021: creators get a warning when their effects could trigger photosensitive epilepsy, and viewers can skip photosensitive content. — [TikTok Newsroom (Sept 2021)](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/auto-captions-on-tiktok); [Stuff](https://stuff.co.za/2021/04/06/tiktok-adds-auto-captions/)
- Wellbeing tools (2022–2023): break reminders let users set custom limits on uninterrupted use. Daily-limit options were 40/60/90/120 minutes. Under-18 accounts get a 60-minute daily limit by default, after which a passcode is needed (a guardian's for under-13s). Teens who spend over 100 minutes are prompted to set a limit. The 60-minute figure was chosen with Boston Children's Hospital's Digital Wellness Lab. TikTok said prompts raised use of screen-time tools by 234% in a test. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/tiktok-screen-time-breaks-dashboard-070132244.html); [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2022/06/09/tech/tiktok-time-spent); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230302-tiktok-will-limit-daily-screen-time)
- EU DSA: formal proceedings opened on 19 February 2024. On 6 February 2026 the Commission preliminarily found that TikTok's "infinite scroll", "autoplay", "push notifications" and highly personalised recommender "fuel the urge to keep scrolling and shift the brain of users into 'autopilot mode'". It said "The time management tools do not seem to be effective", that "TikTok needs to change the basic design of its service", and suggested disabling infinite scroll over time, effective screen-time breaks including at night, and adapting the recommender. These findings are preliminary and not final. — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-addictive-design-breach-digital-services-act)
- TikTok called the findings "categorically false and entirely meritless" and will contest them. The maximum fine is 6% of global turnover. Regulators cited extended night-time use by children as a sign of compulsion. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/02/06/tiktoks-addictive-design-breaches-eu-law-commission-says); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366639072/European-Commission-TikToks-addictive-design-breaches-EU-law)
- As of a May 2026 European Parliament think-tank briefing, the investigation was ongoing. The Commission has also opened an addictive-design case against Meta. — [EPRS](https://epthinktank.eu/2026/05/06/addictive-design-on-online-platforms/); [Slaughter and May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102nbs3/meta-joins-tiktok-in-the-commissions-crosshairs-over-addictive-design-under-th)
- US: on 8 October 2024 a coalition of 14 AGs (led by California's Bonta and New York's James) sued. The complaints allege users cannot disable autoplay, that beauty filters harm self-image and were kept because they increase use, and that the business model maximises time spent. TikTok called the suits "incredibly disappointing". — [California AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-attorney-general-james-lead-coalition-suing-tiktok); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/states-sue-tiktok-saying-it-exploits-and-harms-young-users-100824.html)
- US ownership context: in December 2025 TikTok signed agreements for a US joint venture (Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX at 15% each, ByteDance at 19.9%), targeted to close on 22 January 2026. The recommender would be retrained on US data in Oracle's cloud, with a memo promising users "the same experience as today". I did not confirm the close. — [NBC](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/national-international/tiktok-sale-oracle-silver-lake-mgx/4031552/)
- Web constraint: browsers allow muted autoplay, but autoplay with sound needs a user interaction (Chrome desktop also uses a Media Engagement Index). A `play()` call that is blocked rejects with `NotAllowedError`. An AudioContext created before a gesture must be resumed after one (Chrome 71+). — [Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)

### Inferences
- "Sound on by default" can't be copied directly on the web. A TNL focus view has to begin with a tap ("Enter the listening room" or "Start voting"). That first tap unlocks sound for the session, and every later swipe can then play with audio. This friction also happens to be a consent moment, which works ethically in TNL's favour.
- Design to stay clear of the DSA and AG critique: give focus views an end (the 8 entries in this bracket round, today's 12 new tracks, the queue), show "You're all caught up", don't autoplay into an endless recommender, don't send streak or "come back" pushes, and offer a visible pause and a tap-to-mute that persists. TNL's ranking is not a personalised recommender, which is a structural advantage.
- Copy the accessibility features: captions on video posts (the creator types or edits them, stored as WebVTT `<track>`), honouring `prefers-reduced-motion` (already TNL policy), and a flashing-content flag on uploads that blurs the piece until tapped.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative source on TikTok's haptics, double-tap-to-like implementation, long-press menu contents, or the comment sheet staying over a playing video (the long-press "Not interested" claim came only from a low-quality page). These are observable app behaviour but uncited.
- I found no final EU decision or commitments on addictive design as of the sources found (latest is May 2026), and no 2025–2026 outcome of the US state suits in the sources retrieved.

## 3. Trend and remix culture: sounds, Duet/Stitch, challenges, credit

### Takeaway
On TikTok the sound is the unit of virality. About 85% of videos carry music, every use of a sound is gathered on its page, and following a sound is how viewers enter a meme. Remix tools (Duet, Stitch) and caption credit build attribution chains automatically. Trends spread through imitation more than through friendship ("imitation publics"), which turns old or independent songs into chart hits. Credit has been the persistent weak spot (the Renegade episode), and TikTok's fix was an opt-in crediting tool.

### Cited Findings
- Pex analysed TikTok content from January 2019 to December 2023: 84.63% of TikTok videos contained music (up more than 15% since 2019), compared with YouTube at 84%, Instagram 58% and Facebook 49%. 31% of tracks used were speed- or pitch-modified. Method: automated AI classification. Caveat: UMG's catalogue was pulled from TikTok around publication (early 2024). — [Pex](https://pex.com/blog/2023-tiktok-data-report-85-of-videos-on-tiktok-contain-music/); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/02/09/tiktok-music-study-february-2024/)
- "Often the music cue is synonymous with a meme". A user who taps a sound and watches other videos using it reveals taste, and a familiar sound lets creators pace punchlines because viewers anticipate the payoff. — [Eugene Wei](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2020/9/18/seeing-like-an-algorithm)
- Academic framing: Zulli and Zulli argue TikTok's design (sign-up, default page, icons, editing tools, creation norms) promotes mimesis, and they define "imitation publics" as people connected through a shared practice of imitating and replicating content rather than through friend ties. — [New Media & Society, DOI 10.1177/1461444820983603](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461444820983603)
- (Historical, 2020) Stitch launched on 3 September 2020: you clip up to 5 seconds of another video into your own, and every Stitch automatically credits the original creator in the caption with a link to the clipped video. Creators can disable Stitch globally or per video. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-on-tiktok-introducing-stitch)
- (Historical, 2020) Duet got new layouts (left/right, react, top/bottom, three-screen). Duets credit and link the original creator in the caption, with the same opt-out toggles. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/feature-highlight-new-layouts-for-duet?lang=en); [Newsroom UK](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-gb/new-layouts-for-tiktok-duets-launch-in-europe)
- (Historical, 2022) Creator crediting tool (May 2022): on the posting page you tap a video icon and pick a video you liked, favourited, posted, or that used the same sound. It is added as a mention in the caption and the credited creator gets an Inbox alert. It covers "dances, jokes, viral sounds and more" but "still relies on user adoption". — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/18/tiktok-launches-its-first-creator-crediting-tool-to-help-video-creators-cite-their-inspiration)
- (Historical, 2019–2020) Renegade: choreographed by 14-year-old Jalaiah Harmon to K Camp's "Lottery" (Sept 2019). It spread via bigger creators without credit until a NYT story in 2020, after which she performed at the NBA All-Star Game. Her post shows the "original sound" label attributing audio to its first uploader. — [Wikipedia: Lottery (K Camp song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery_(K_Camp_song)); [Her Campus](https://www.hercampus.com/?p=1177086)
- (Historical, 2018–2019) "Old Town Road": self-released by Lil Nas X on 3 December 2018, spread via TikTok's "Yeehaw Challenge" (clips that switch into western gear when the beat drops, "Yee Yee Juice"), and was removed from Billboard's country chart (which drew attention). Remixes (Billy Ray Cyrus, "Seoul Town Road" with RM, Diplo, Young Thug, Mason Ramsey) kept it going to a record-breaking 17th week at No. 1 by late July 2019. The final 19-week total is widely reported but was not verified in the sources retrieved. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/music/lil-nas-x-s-old-town-road-breaks-record-most-n1035686); [Consequence](https://consequence.net/2019/07/lil-nas-x-old-town-road-no-1-record)
- (Historical, 2020) Fleetwood Mac "Dreams": Nathan Apodaca's (420doggface208) skateboard/cranberry-juice clip went up on 25 September 2020. Over 25–27 September the song had 2.9M US on-demand streams (+88.7%) and 3,000 downloads (+374%), and it re-entered the Hot 100 at No. 21 for the week of 17 October. Mick Fleetwood and Stevie Nicks then posted their own versions, an example of the original artist joining the remix chain. — [Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/tiktok-fleetwood-mac-song-dreams-streaming-service-charts); [WINK News](https://winknews.com/2020/10/22/fleetwood-macs-dreams-returns-to-the-charts-thanks-to-viral-tiktok-video/); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/mick-fleetwood-joined-tiktok-to-recreate-the-viral-video-that-caused-fleetwood-macs-dreams-to-surge-in-popularity)
- Music impact (TikTok-commissioned Luminate report, Feb 2025): 84% of songs entering the Billboard Global 200 in 2024 went viral on TikTok first, and an artist can expect an average 11% rise in on-demand streams in the 3 days after a peak in TikTok views. TikTok's own year-end report said 13 of 16 US Hot 100 No. 1s in 2024 were "linked to a trend" on TikTok. Caveat: the report is TikTok-commissioned and "linked" is not the same as caused. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-and-luminate-release-latest-music-impact-report); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/tiktok-84-of-songs-that-entered-billboards-global-200-chart-in-2024-went-viral-on-our-platform-first/)
- (Historical, 2019) Branded challenge: Chipotle's #GuacDance (built on an existing viral song) drew 250,000+ videos and about 430M video starts in 6 days, and Chipotle called it TikTok's top US branded challenge. It was tied to a real-world offer (free guac on 31 July). Figures are self-reported. — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/chipotle-smashes-tiktok-records-with-guacdance-challenge/560102/); [Chipotle](https://newsroom.chipotle.com/2019-08-01-Chipotles-National-Avocado-Day-Celebrations-Smash-Records)
- "Add Yours" is a prompt sticker: a creator sets a theme and viewers reply with their own content. There was no official TikTok launch post found. Instagram (late 2021) and YouTube Shorts (rolled out to all users September 2024) copied the format. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/what-is-the-add-yours-feature-on-tiktok-2222994/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/11/youtube-shorts-collaborative-add-yours-sticker-is-now-available-to-all-users/)

### Inferences
- The core mechanic for TNL is the sound page: one page per sound or beat that lists every post using it, with the original credited at the top and a "Use this sound" button. TNL already awards +4 rep to the sound owner when someone builds with it, so the page makes visible a rep flow that already exists. It doubles as a remix chain and gives the most direct answer to "how did this spread" in a small community.
- Attribution should be automatic, not opt-in. TikTok's crediting tool shows that voluntary credit underperforms. TNL can store `parent_post_id` / `sound_id` on creation and render "built on @name's sound" without asking. It should also offer the creator-side controls TikTok has (allow remixes on/off per post), and remixes should still respect the collab rule (collabs count only once accepted; a remix is credit, not a collab).
- Trends need a template plus a deadline. Old Town Road, Dreams and GuacDance all paired a recognisable audio cue with a simple, repeatable action. TNL's weekly prompt can be "this beat + this brief + this week", posted as an event or a lightweight "Add yours" thread whose responses gather on one page. Tie it to something real (a feature, the Showroom, a prize), as GuacDance tied to guac.
- Credit can be a social good as well as a rule. The Renegade case is the cautionary tale a creatives-first app can position against ("every chain shows its origin").

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous, citable data on trend lifecycle length (inception to peak to decline). Search turned up only low-quality pages, and the claim needs academic or Pex/Chartmetric data.
- I found no official TikTok source on templates (CapCut/photo templates) or on the "Add Yours" launch date.

## 4. What TNL can borrow (and avoid), with vanilla-JS implementation

### Takeaway
Borrow four things: (1) a sound/beat page with automatic credit and the existing +4 rep; (2) remix and answer chains; (3) weekly prompt formats; (4) an opt-in, bounded, one-at-a-time focus mode for the Music lab and event voting, with swipe-to-next and a pick button. All of it can be built with CSS scroll-snap, IntersectionObserver, and `preload` and `play()` handling, with no dependencies, using transform/opacity only. Avoid endless autoplay, re-engagement pushes and hidden "not interested" controls, which are exactly what regulators are targeting.

### Cited Findings
- `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` on the container plus `scroll-snap-align: start` on children creates vertical pages. `scroll-snap-stop: always` stops a fast fling from skipping past a page, and the difference is most noticeable with `mandatory`. `mandatory` can trap users if a page is taller than the viewport. — [MDN scroll-snap-stop](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/scroll-snap-stop); [webreference](https://webreference.com/css/properties/scroll-snap-type/)
- Muted autoplay is allowed, sound needs a gesture, a rejected `play()` raises `NotAllowedError`, and an AudioContext needs `resume()` after a gesture. — [Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)
- Background loading of the next item is part of why TikTok's swipe feels instant. — [Eugene Wei](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2020/9/18/seeing-like-an-algorithm)
- Regulators named infinite scroll, autoplay and push notifications as the risky features, and found opt-in time tools ineffective. — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-addictive-design-breach-digital-services-act)

### Inferences: implementable patterns (vanilla JS, no new dependencies)
1. Focus pager (Music lab "Listen one by one", event "Vote one by one", `EVFOCUS` landing). Use a fixed overlay `.focus{position:fixed;inset:0;overflow-y:auto;scroll-snap-type:y mandatory;overscroll-behavior:contain}` with children `.focus>section{height:100dvh;scroll-snap-align:start;scroll-snap-stop:always}`. Use `100dvh` so mobile toolbars don't break pages. The list is finite (queue, round, today's tracks) and the last section is "All caught up — back to the lab". Prefer this over a swipe-gesture handler, because native scrolling gives momentum, accessibility and the back-swipe for free, and it moves no layout, which fits the glitch-watcher rule.
2. Active-item detection: `new IntersectionObserver(cb,{root:pager,threshold:0.6})` on each section. On `isIntersecting`, call `media.play().catch(()=>showTapToPlay())` on the active item and `pause()` plus a reset on the others. Store `currentIndex` for vote and "next" buttons. Expose `aria-roledescription="slide"` and arrow-key and PageDown support on desktop (from 1024px TNL uses the computer frame, where a centred 9:16 column with up/down buttons beats wheel-snapping).
3. Sound unlock: the overlay opens from a tap ("Start listening"). Inside that handler, call `play()` on the first item unmuted and resume any shared AudioContext (Studio/player), so later items can autoplay with sound. Keep one global mute toggle that persists. Remembering it in `localStorage` is fine because it is a per-viewer convenience; wrap it in try/catch.
4. Prefetch: for index ±1 set `preload="auto"` (or create a detached `new Audio(url)` with `preload='auto'`), and leave others at `preload="none"` with a poster image so the first frame is instant. Unload by removing `src` beyond ±2 to cap memory on phones. Use `skel()` for the poster until `loadeddata`.
5. Spinning-record cue for "a sound is playing": a rotating disc with `animation: spin 4s linear infinite` (transform only), paused when media pauses and under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Tapping it opens the sound page. A marquee sound title should be a `transform: translateX` animation, not a `left` change.
6. Double-tap to like (or to "pick" in events). Use `pointerup` timing (<300 ms between taps, under 30 px apart) to trigger the like plus a heart/check burst using scale/opacity, and keep single-tap as pause. Add `touch-action: manipulation` to avoid zoom delay. For haptics, `navigator.vibrate?.(10)` works on Android and is a no-op on iOS Safari, so treat it as optional.
7. Swipe voting in events: for poll and bracket rounds, show each entry full-screen with one "Pick" button. For bracket pairs, show a two-up screen and swipe to the next pair. Keep TNL's rules: counts hidden, no self-vote, server-enforced. Don't make "skip" a negative vote, because TikTok reads a swipe as disinterest, and that would make vote order bias results. Randomise order per voter on the server.
8. Sound/beat page `/s/:id`: the original at the top ("original sound — @owner"), then "Built on this" as a grid of every post with that `sound_id` (newest or most-liked), a count, and a "Use this sound" button that opens the post creator with the sound preselected. Each use writes the existing +4 rep event to the owner (already in `REP`). Opt-out per sound: "allow others to build on this".
9. Answer chains: a post can be "in answer to" another post. Store `parent_post_id`, and the caption renders "↳ answering @name" automatically (the Stitch/Duet caption-credit model). The parent shows a "N answers" row that opens the chain in focus mode.
10. Weekly prompt ("Add yours"): an admin- or member-created prompt (brief + optional beat + end date). Responses gather on the prompt page, and the best ones flow into a tournament or a Showroom feature. Run it as a light alternative to a full event.
11. Seek bar only on long media: render the progress bar only if `duration > 30` (TikTok shows it on longer videos). Update it with `transform: scaleX(t/duration)` rather than width.
12. Captions: a `<track kind="captions">` for video posts, with creator-typed text. Add an upload checkbox "contains flashing lights" that shows a tap-to-reveal cover (photosensitivity, after TikTok's 2021 warning).

What to avoid: infinite autoplay into an algorithmic feed, autoplay that can't be paused, streak or re-engagement pushes, night-time nudges to keep watching, and hiding "not for me" / mute controls (the ICWSM finding). Also avoid copying TikTok's cyan/red glitch or the trademarked note.

### Gaps
- I didn't measure performance budgets (for example, how many preloaded media elements mobile Safari tolerates). This needs an on-device test in TNL's `npm run e2e`.
- I found no published TikTok data on small-community remix rates. Lessons transfer by analogy from a very large platform.
