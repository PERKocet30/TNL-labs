# Make the offer the engine, not the fee

Depop's most useful lesson for TNL Labs is about **how prices move**, not how fees are charged. Over 40% of Depop items sell through offers, at an average 23% discount, and sellers send about 2M targeted offers a week to people who liked or bagged an item. TNL already has the parts this needs (offers, saves, price-drop alerts, a bag with one checkout per seller), but its offers are accept/decline only and never expire. The second lesson is that **protection lives where the money flows**. Depop protects buyers only inside its own payment rail and protects sellers only when they upload tracking. TNL has the rail (Stripe Connect Standard, direct charges) but none of the plumbing: no webhook, no dispute state, refunds only from the seller's Stripe dashboard, and a delivery-rep award that one buyer tap can mint. Depop's 2024 fee change is a warning, not a model. Moving the 10% seller fee to a buyer "marketplace fee" of up to 5% + $1 lifted listings 30–45% in a month, but the platform's take barely changed. Depop then **bought growth with brand spend, lost $196.6M pre-tax in H1 2026**, was sold by Etsy to eBay for about $1.4B against the $1.6B Etsy paid, and now faces a drip-pricing class action. TNL's market should build, in this order: trustworthy order plumbing (webhook, rep tied to money outcomes, stock holds), then offer mechanics (counteroffers, expiry, offer-to-savers), then cheap discovery (style tags, saved searches) and bundle pricing. It should not copy paid Boost, buyer fees added at checkout, or growth paid for with marketing.

## Depop sold 59% of its sellers something back, then sold itself at a loss

Depop's history explains which of its choices are durable. Simon Beckerman founded it in 2011 as an online flea market growing out of PIG, the fashion magazine he co-founded ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depop)). Etsy bought it in June 2021 for about $1.6B. At the end of 2025 Depop had **55.6M registered users, 7.0M active buyers (+37.7%) and 3.2M active sellers (+41.1%)**. 87% of buyers were under 34, 93% of GMS was apparel, and **59% of sellers who made a sale in 2025 also bought something** ([Etsy FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)). That last number is the one TNL should care about most. It is the signature of a community marketplace rather than a store: the same people sell and buy, and social activity (85M likes, follows and messages a month as early as 2019) feeds the loop ([General Atlantic](https://www.generalatlantic.com/media-article/depop-raises-62-million-series-c-to-meet-rising-demand-from-us-gen-z/)).

Growth then accelerated in a way that was paid for. GMS went from about $789M in 2024 to **$1,074.9M in 2025 (+36.3%)**, with Q4 US buyer GMS up 60.2% on Depop's "largest-ever brand marketing campaign" ([Etsy FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)). Q2 2026 GMS was $455.7M, up 83%, but the **pre-tax loss that quarter was $160.8M against $17.8M a year earlier**, and H1 2026 operating cash burn was $147.1M against $11.5M ([Etsy Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001370637/000137063726000079/q226shareholderletter.htm)). Etsy agreed to sell Depop to eBay on 15 February 2026 for $1.2B and closed on 30 July 2026 for about $1.4B including adjustments, with proceeds going to buybacks ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/ebay-completes-1-4-billion-dollar-depop-acquisition/2026073073828)). eBay says Depop will run separately and keep its brand and culture, while eBay looks for synergies in shipping, compliance, personalisation and "trusted services" ([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/ebay-closes-its-1-4b-depop-acquisition-and-says-the-resale-marketplace-will-keep-its-own-brand-platform-and-culture/)). There is no primary detail yet on eBay-era fees or ranking.

A fair reading of the deal is that the culture was worth buying and the economics were not yet working. The roughly ninefold jump in quarterly losses alongside an 83% GMS rise suggests Etsy decided not to keep funding a loss-making second marketplace. That is an inference, but it is consistent with Etsy's own statement that the proceeds "further accelerate our stock buyback program". The counter-example is Vinted: ten times Depop's GMV, **€1.1B revenue and €62M net profit in 2025**, on a buyer-protection fee with no seller commission ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/vinted-reaches-1-1-billion-euros-in-revenue-for-2025-accepting-a-strategic-drop-in-profits/2026040971654)). So buyer-paid fees can be profitable at scale. Depop's losses came from spending, not from the fee model as such.

For TNL, the trajectory gives three practical rules. Grow the sell-and-buy loop through the social layer TNL already has, and measure it: seller-to-buyer conversion should be an admin stat, since Depop's 59% shows what a healthy number looks like. Do not buy growth before the unit economics hold. And treat the culture as the asset: eBay paid for a brand with a scene, not for software.

## Moving the fee to buyers changed who sees it, not how much is taken

Before 2024 every Depop seller paid 10%. The UK dropped it for new listings from 20 March 2024 and added a buyer fee from 15 April. The US followed on 15–18 July 2024 with a **buyer "marketplace fee" of up to 5% of the item price plus up to $1**, with payment processing still charged ([Depop newsroom (US)](https://news.depop.com/depop-removes-selling-fees-in-the-united-states-evolves-fee-structure/); [Depop newsroom (UK)](https://news.depop.com/evolving-our-fee-structure-with-zero-selling-fees-on-depop/)). Australia followed on 22 July 2026, and that announcement confirms Depop Payments is "powered by Stripe" ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/depop-drops-seller-fees-australia/)). Depop's own claimed result is **a 30–45% increase in listings in the 28 days after each change** ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/depop-drops-seller-fees-australia/)). Mercari made a similar move earlier in 2024 ([ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2024/07/depop-removes-selling-fees-in-the-united-states/)).

The arithmetic matters more than the headline. On a typical $25–40 item, up to 5% + $1 is roughly 7.5–9% of the item price. Add the 8% attributed Boost fee on some sales, and Depop's take rate is probably still around 8–11% of GMS, much the same as the old 10% seller fee. This is an estimate, because Depop's dollar revenue is not public in the sources found. The visible "0%" brought sellers in. It did not make the marketplace cheaper. Commentators warned at the time that a visible buyer fee pushes list prices down and squeezes margins anyway ([Money](https://money.com/depop-seller-fee-eliminated/?amp=true)). In September 2026 a proposed class action alleged "drip pricing", meaning the fee appears only at checkout. Only the headline is available, so its merits are unknown ([fluf.io](https://fluf.io/seller-news/category/depop)).

TNL's model is the opposite, and on this evidence it is the better one for TNL. `FEE_BY_LEVEL = {1:10, 2:8, 3:6, 4:4, 5:2}` in `src/db.js` charges the seller, scales the fee down as rep from other people's actions rises, and takes it as `application_fee_amount` on the item only. Depop's lesson is about **presentation**. Show sellers what they keep ("You keep 90% → 98% as your rep rises") with the same prominence Depop gives "0% selling fees". Show buyers one all-in price (item plus shipping) on the card and in the bag, which also targets Baymard's top checkout-abandonment reasons: extra costs (40%) and total not visible up front (12%) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)). If `FEE_BY_LEVEL` ever changes, copy the one thing Depop did well procedurally: announce in advance and apply the change to new listings only. If TNL ever adds a buyer-side charge, follow Vinted rather than Depop: call it protection, make it pay for something real, and show it on the listing. The fee comparison against Etsy and Depop is already in the Instagram lessons report and is not repeated here.

## Offers move 40% of Depop's sales, and TNL's stop at accept or decline

Depop's offer data is the strongest number in this research. Make Offer launched in 2022 and seller-sent "Send Offer" followed in 2023. By January 2024 **over 40% of items sold through offers, buyers saved 23% on average, 62M offers had been made, and sellers sent about 2M offers a week** to people who had liked or bagged an item. A buyer has **24 hours to buy or counter** a seller's offer before it expires ([Depop newsroom](https://news.depop.com/depop-community-embraces-offers-as-suite-of-negotiation-tools-expands/)). Depop's Selling API even automates offers to likers and baggers ([Depop Partner API](https://partnerapi.depop.com/api-docs/how-to-guides/managing-offers/)). Negotiation is a social act on Depop, and the like is the audience for it.

TNL's offer flow (`src/server-06-settings-payouts-market.js:352-388`) lets a buyer offer below asking, and lets the seller accept or decline. Accepting declines every other pending offer and tells the buyer "go buy it". There is no counter, no expiry and no seller-initiated offer. Reading the checkout code adds a gap the notes did not flag. `/api/market/:id/buy` (`src/server-07-orders-sharing.js:16-18`) and `/api/cart/checkout` (`src/server-07-cart.js:40`) both price a line at the buyer's **latest accepted offer with no time limit**, and nothing marks the offer used after a purchase. On a multi-unit listing, an offer accepted once keeps applying to every later purchase by that buyer, indefinitely. That makes expiry and single use a correctness fix as well as a feature.

The build follows directly. **Counteroffers with a 24-hour expiry**: an offer becomes a short thread (buyer offers, seller counters, buyer accepts or counters, a maximum of about three rounds). The accepted price locks server-side, expires after 24 hours, and is consumed by exactly one order. **Offer to savers**: TNL already treats saves as purchase intent, since `listing_likes` drives the price-drop alert in `src/server-06-market-stock.js:74-75`. A seller-set "X% off for savers" (minimum 10%, one send per listing per week) would create a private 24-hour offer for each saver through the existing `notify()`. That is Depop's 2M-a-week mechanic using data TNL already holds. Neither needs a dependency. Both change the amount sent to Stripe, so both need `/security-review`. Neither touches rep, which keeps the rule that rep comes only from what others do.

Bundles are the other price lever. Depop's bundles are mostly a shipping saving: one checkout per seller and one label, plus custom bundle listings held for one buyer ([OneShop](https://tools.oneshop.com/blog/bundles-in-depop)). A percentage bundle discount is claimed only by a vendor blog ([Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/platform-specific-guides/how-do-bundles-work-on-depop)). TNL's bag already has Depop's structure, so a seller setting for "X% off 2+ items" and/or "free shipping over $Y", computed in `server-07-cart.js`, finishes the job. This is where Depop and Shopify agree. Shopify's native discounts are amount-off, buy-X-get-Y and free shipping, with anything fancier pushed to apps ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts/discount-types)). Small, server-computed, per-seller rules are the right size, and commission should be calculated on the discounted price.

## Discovery runs on aesthetic, curation and freshness, and Boost is the part to refuse

Depop shoppers browse a personalised feed and search by vibe (#y2k, #grunge) as much as by category. Hashtags and backend "style" attributes feed that search ([List Perfectly](https://listperfectly.com/?p=11184); [OneShop](https://tools.oneshop.com/blog/style-tags-depop)). Curation carries a lot of discovery. 2026 brought a Gabriella Karefa-Johnson curated shop, Spotify artist-curated shops and an official Coachtopia shop ([fluf.io](https://fluf.io/seller-news/category/depop)). In September 2025 Depop launched **Outfits**: shoppable mood-board collages where every item is buyable, a sold item triggers "see similar", and the boards share to Pinterest and Instagram ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/depop-launches-a-fashion-collaging-tool-to-style-pinterest-worthy-outfits/)).

TNL's market search is a SQL `LIKE` over title, description and brand, with fixed categories and filters. It has a similar-items query (same category, 0.4–2.2× price) and "Shop this post". The cheap wins map cleanly. **Style tags**: up to about five per listing, a search clause and a chip row, in one or two days with no dependency. **Saved searches with alerts**: store query parameters, match them on listing create, and notify. This is the same pattern as price-drop and the Shopify notes' back-in-stock waitlist. Depop's native saved-search alerts are confirmed only by a third-party guide ([Sagedatum](https://sagedatum.com/blogs/apps/how-do-i-find-items-to-buy-on-depop)), but the habit is common across resale apps. **Multi-seller styled boards**: extend "Shop this post" to tag up to about six listings from any seller, reuse the similar-items query for "sold, see similar", and share through the existing share card. This is Outfits for a creative app. Because a creator styling someone else's piece gives that seller reach, it fits TNL's rep philosophy without any new rep kind. A "From people you follow" market filter and an admin-curated Picks shelf with stable IDs cover the curation lesson.

Two Depop patterns should be designed against. The first is **Boost**: a labelled extra placement in search and "Suggested for you", charged at 8% (US/AU) or 12% (UK, sources conflict) only when a buyer who interacted with the boost buys within 28 days ([Depop Partner API](https://partnerapi.depop.com/api-docs/concepts/boosting/); [Crosslist](https://crosslist.com/blog/boost-items-on-depop)). Depop claims boosting "can increase selling chances by 25%" ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/depop-product-release-june-2025/)). The attribution model is clever, and the "additional slot, never replacing organic" rule is fair, but it is still pay-to-rank, which conflicts with TNL's no-paid-reach stance and tilts the feed against small creatives. TNL's version should be earned: a rep-weighted shelf, not a paid one. The second is **bump culture**. Depop publishes no ranking formula, sellers believe recency rules, and refresh bots exist to exploit it ([Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/beginner-guides-how-tos/how-to-boost-listing-on-depop-2025-seller-s-guide-from-real-experience); [Resellify Bump](https://chromeboard.com/extension/depop-bot-resellify-bump-noigpihfhadeeakhllpgnfomabmghbpd)). TNL should never let edits reset `created_at`, should keep price-drop tags as the honest freshness signal, and should publish its ranking rule in plain words.

On the listing side, Depop's AI fills description, category, colour and brand from one photo, and "almost half of listers" tried it in testing ([Depop newsroom](https://news.depop.com/depop-launches-ai-powered-listing-from-one-photo/)). Photoroom handles background clean-up ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/depop-product-release-june-2025/)). Both are external services and need owner approval. The no-dependency stand-in is a photo checklist in the Media step (plain-background cover, a worn or styled shot, flaws close up, a label shot) and pre-filling fields from the seller's last listing. Depop's engineering confirms the order to invest in: its recommendations started as a nightly Spark batch on EMR before moving toward real-time serving ([Depop Engineering](https://engineering.depop.com/show-the-people-what-they-want-without-breaking-the-bank-e953b79da84d)), and its named teams are Search & Retrieval, Better Matching, Experimentation and Trust Detection ML ([Built In London](https://builtinlondon.uk/job/backend-engineer-search-and-retrieval/10762845)). For TNL, a nightly ranking pass in SQLite is a legitimate first stage, not a shortcut.

## Protection only works inside the rail, and TNL's rail has no return wire

Depop's trust design fits in one sentence. **Buyers are covered only if they pay through Depop Payments**, for items that are not received, damaged or significantly not as described, if reported within 30 days. Refunds cover item, shipping, fees and tax. PayPal, bank transfer and meetups are excluded ([Depop Help](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360038461713-Depop-Protection-for-buyers)). **Sellers are covered when they upload valid tracking**, and sellers report losing disputes when tracking was late or didn't show delivery ([Depop Help: sellers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001845367-Depop-Protection-for-sellers)). The 2026 Terms add auto-cancel and refund for orders not shipped in a reasonable time ([List Perfectly](https://listperfectly.com/selling/depop-terms-of-service-update-2026/)). That one rule makes off-platform payment the dominant scam: Venmo or CashApp requests, fake "payment sent" screenshots, phishing links ([NordProtect](https://nordprotect.com/blog/depop-scams)). In 2020, buyers tricked into paying directly led to forced password resets and MFA ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depop)). Counterfeits are handled reactively by an ML Trust Detection team plus community reports, with no formal authentication step ([Built In London](https://builtinlondon.uk/job/senior-machine-learning-scientist/11079419); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/a97bb06c-9fbd-4510-b8cd-d1b6f96f9a4b/)). Shopify Protect makes the same trade from the other side: free fraud-chargeback cover, but only if the merchant fulfils with tracking within 7 days ([Shopify Protect](https://www.shopify.com/protect)). Both platforms tie protection to shipping with real tracking.

TNL already says the right thing in code: the buy route refuses to fall back to DMs because platform checkout is "the only way the buyer has any protection". The plumbing behind that promise is missing, and the verified code facts show where. `POST /api/orders/:id/received` (`src/server-07-orders-sharing.js:186-196`) awards the seller **+10 `delivery_confirmed` rep on a single buyer tap**, with only a paid-order check. There is no tracking verification and no time window, and the rep is not revoked on refund. Tracking is a free-text string of up to 80 characters. **Stock is not reserved at checkout** (lines 111-116): two buyers can pay for the last unit, and the seller is told to refund one from their Stripe dashboard. And **`src/pay.js` has no Stripe webhook**, so refunds and disputes never reach the app. Together these break both halves of Depop's model. The buyer has no claim window or dispute state. The seller gains nothing from uploading tracking. Rep, which lowers fees permanently, can be minted by a sockpuppet buyer and then refunded. The webhook and the refund-proof sale rep are already TNL's top items in the code-vs-Instagram report (P0-3) and the Instagram backend report (P0-5). Depop's evidence adds the order-lifecycle layer on top of them.

That layer has five parts, all buildable without a new dependency. First, a **dispute window**: `delivered`/`complete` stays open for issues for 30 days from purchase, and the buyer can raise `issue_open` with a reason (not received, damaged, not as described, counterfeit) and photos. Second, **`delivery_confirmed` rep only after the window closes clean** (or after a verified carrier delivery), revoked through the existing `revokeRep` on any refund or lost dispute. Third, a **ship-by deadline** shown on the listing ("Ships in N days"), with reminders at 48 hours and buyer-cancel-for-refund after the deadline plus grace. Fourth, **in-app refunds** through `POST /v1/refunds` on the connected account with `refund_application_fee=true` so TNL returns its cut. Stripe's refund semantics for Standard direct charges should be confirmed in its docs before building. Fifth, a **stock hold** for the life of the Stripe session, released by the existing stale-pending sweeper, which removes the oversell path entirely. A structured tracking field (carrier dropdown plus number, rendered as a carrier link) makes "tracking protects the seller" enforceable without a shipping API. On the scam side, a regex warning chip in market DMs for "venmo", "cashapp", "paypal f&f", emails and phone numbers copies Depop's main defence for the price of a few lines in `server-10-dm-core.js`. Counterfeit controls stay rules-based: a report reason, an admin queue, keyword flags ("rep", "1:1", "replica", "dupe"), and a required label or receipt photo above a price threshold.

One structural limit should be stated plainly. Depop and Vinted can hold payouts because they control the money. TNL's direct charges on Standard accounts settle straight to the seller, so TNL **cannot escrow**. It can only refund on the seller's account and claw back rep. Moving to destination charges with Express accounts would allow a transfer after delivery, but it is a large payments, cost and legal change. The recommendation is to stay on Standard and make the rep ledger, not the money, carry the platform's leverage, because rep sets the fee tier. Depop's "sole discretion, not an insurance policy" fine print ([Depop Help](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001772568-Buying-safely-on-Depop)) is also worth not copying. TNL's protection promise should be short, specific and shown at checkout.

Each P0 and P1 item needs a test that fails on today's behaviour, for example: accepted offer used twice → second purchase at sticker price; refunded order → `sale_made` and `delivery_confirmed` reversed; two concurrent checkouts for the last unit → one is refused. Changes to how the market feels (counter sheet, bundle total updating in the bag) need a step in `test/e2e/run.mjs`.

**What not to copy.** Paid Boost and Boost Shop: an attributed 8–12% fee is still pay-to-rank, and it creates a two-tier feed that works against small creatives. Hidden or late buyer fees: the take barely falls, sellers' margins get squeezed anyway, and the practice is now in court. Growth bought with brand spend: Depop's 83% GMS quarter came with a $160.8M loss and a sale below its purchase price. Recency ranking that rewards bumping and bots. A minimum listing volume for status (Depop's Top Seller requires 50 new listings a month), which rewards resellers rather than creatives. TNL's levels already do the job. A stored-value wallet (Depop Balance) or gift cards, which raise money-transmission questions TNL's direct-charge design deliberately avoids. And "sole discretion" protection wording.

## Conclusion

The research changes where TNL's market risk sits. The question was what to add, but the evidence says TNL first has to make the existing promise true. Depop's whole trust model is one rule (pay here, ship tracked, report within 30 days), and TNL has the rule in its code comments without the wiring. Because TNL lets rep set the commission, a gap that only costs Depop money costs TNL its fee schedule's integrity: a refunded self-purchase earns a cheaper fee tier for good. Fixing the webhook, the delivery-rep timing, offer expiry and the stock hold is about a week of work with no new dependency. It also turns rep into what Depop's Top Seller badge tries to be, a reliability signal backed by money outcomes.

After that, Depop points TNL toward negotiation and curation rather than fees and paid reach. Its best mechanic (offers to the people who saved an item) runs on data TNL already collects. Its best culture product (Outfits) is a natural extension of "Shop this post". And its most expensive lesson, buying growth and selling below cost, argues for TNL's slower model: a seller fee that falls as other people vouch for your work. The open questions are empirical. There are no public Depop dispute, fraud or counterfeit rates to size TNL's protection against, eBay's plans for Depop fees and authentication are unknown, and Stripe's refund behaviour on Standard direct charges should be checked against current docs before item 5 is built.
