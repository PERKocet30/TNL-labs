# Make every sound a page others build on

*Research synthesis, 8 October 2026. It draws on five research notes in `research_notes/TikTok lessons for TNL Labs/` and builds on the three Instagram reports in `reports/`. Repo facts come from reading the code on branch `research/tiktok-lessons`. One assumption is treated as already shipped: DM sends in the Showroom ranking (PR #36, `sendExtra` 0.75). That PR is open, not merged, so this branch's `src/server-10-rank.js` does not have it yet.*

TikTok's most transferable lesson for TNL is a creation loop, not its feed. **Every TikTok post is a reusable ingredient.** A sound, a five-second clip or a prompt can each be reused in one tap, the reuse credits and links the source automatically, and a public page gathers every derivative. That builds what scholars call "imitation publics": people connected by copying one another's work rather than by friendship. TNL already holds most of the data for this. `posts.audio_track_id` links a post to a member's track, and a Studio beat records what it was remixed from (`beat.remixOf`). What TNL lacks is the public page, the one-tap reuse button and a fair reward. **The +4 `sound_used` rep is paid only when someone uses a Studio library *sample* through `POST /api/library/:id/use`** (`src/server-08-trust-library.js:239–250`). Putting another member's *track* on your post pays its owner nothing, and no page lists the posts that used a track. The first build is therefore a track page with "Use this sound", plus a once-per-person rep event for the track's owner. Next come TikTok's cheap, deterministic ranking rules: no two cards in a row from the same creator or sound, never re-show what a viewer has seen, a few slots off the viewer's usual labs, and a damp on a lab the viewer has already seen a lot of today. These build on the Instagram report's impression log rather than replacing it. On design, TNL should borrow TikTok's one-piece-at-a-time *focus* for music and event voting, and refuse its bottomlessness. The European Commission found in February 2026, preliminarily, that infinite scroll, autoplay and push notifications breach the Digital Services Act. On money, TikTok's three creator-payout programmes in four years and its coin-based gifting are the strongest argument for TNL's published, falling fee ladder. Everything in the top ten builds needs no new dependency.

## TikTok wins on reuse with credit, and TNL has half the plumbing

TikTok's remix tools share one design: a reuse button on any post, credit attached without asking, and a page that gathers every derivative. Stitch (September 2020) lets a creator "select up to five seconds" of someone else's video to play before their own. **The caption credits the original, and "the caption attribution links directly to the original, clipped video."** Creators can switch Stitch off for all their videos or for one upload ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/new-on-tiktok-introducing-stitch?lang=en)). Duet works the same way, with the same toggles ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/feature-highlight-new-layouts-for-duet?lang=en)). Tapping the audio label on any video opens a sound page listing the videos that reuse it ([SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/how-to-duet-and-stitch-on-tiktok)). The sound is the unit of virality. Pex's automated analysis found **84.63% of TikTok videos contained music** (2019–2023), against 58% on Instagram ([Pex](https://pex.com/blog/2023-tiktok-data-report-85-of-videos-on-tiktok-contain-music/)). Zulli and Zulli argue that TikTok's design as a whole, from sign-up to editing tools, promotes mimesis. They name the resulting networks **"imitation publics"**: people bound by imitation rather than by friend ties ([New Media & Society](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461444820983603)).

Credit is TikTok's persistent weak spot, and how it failed matters to TNL. In 2019 the Renegade dance spread through bigger creators without credit until the 14-year-old who made it was identified in 2020 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery_(K_Camp_song))). TikTok's answer was a voluntary crediting tool (May 2022). TikTok itself conceded that it "still relies on user adoption" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/18/tiktok-launches-its-first-creator-crediting-tool-to-help-video-creators-cite-their-inspiration)). **The lesson is that credit must be structural, not optional.** Stitch credits automatically; the optional tool did not fix credit. Instagram points the same way: it now prefers originals over reposts in recommendations, as the earlier report covers. TikTok goes further. Instagram treats audio as something you attach to a Reel; TikTok makes the sound a destination people visit.

The "build on it" pattern has also produced chart hits. "Old Town Road" spread through the "Yeehaw Challenge", a recognisable audio cue paired with one repeatable action, and remixes kept it at No. 1 for a record 17th week by July 2019 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/music/lil-nas-x-s-old-town-road-breaks-record-most-n1035686)). Nathan Apodaca's skateboard clip in 2020 lifted Fleetwood Mac's "Dreams" by **88.7% in US on-demand streams in three days**, and the band then joined the chain with their own versions ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/tiktok-fleetwood-mac-song-dreams-streaming-service-charts)). TikTok's own figures, from a TikTok-commissioned Luminate report, claim **84% of songs entering the Billboard Global 200 in 2024 went viral on TikTok first**. The same report says "Add to Music App" had passed a billion track saves ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-and-luminate-release-latest-music-impact-report?lang=en)). These are TikTok's numbers, and "went viral first" is not the same as caused. The funnel shape is still clear: a song plays in a post, a tap opens the sound page, others build on it, and the song is saved off-platform.

### What TNL already has, and the correction this report makes

Code reading shows TNL is closer than one of the research notes assumed:

- **Tracks on posts.** `posts.audio_track_id` exists (`src/db.js:508–512`). The feed joins it (`src/server-01-boot.js:317`), and a reshare keeps the sound (`src/server-02-auth-feed.js:345`). The sound chip renders in `src/app-18-media.js:113–115`, and tapping it only plays the track.
- **Beat remix.** The Studio already has a BandLab-style remix. A beat carries `remixOf`, the card shows "from @name", and publishing a remix gives the original author `share_received` (+3) (`src/server-02-auth-feed.js:296–304`). This is hidden unless the Studio is switched on. It also pays once per remix post, not once per person, which is the same faucet the earlier report's P0-2 flagged for shares.
- **Sample rep.** `sound_used` (+4) is awarded only through `POST /api/library/:id/use`, for Studio library samples, once per person per sample. The design-trends note says TNL "already awards +4 rep to the sound owner when someone builds with it". That is true for samples and **false for tracks**. The coordinator confirmed in the code that no rep flows when someone uses your track on their post.
- **What is missing.** There is no `GET` route that lists posts by `audio_track_id`, no "Use this sound" entry from a post, and no lineage for a post that builds on another post with its own new media. `shared_from` covers only reshares.

The adoption verdicts follow directly from this. A **track page with a "Built with this" grid and "Use this sound"** is TikTok's sound page. Paying the track owner the first time each *other* member publishes with their track extends the existing `sound_used` logic. It needs a `track_uses` table that mirrors `sample_uses`, and it keeps the rule that rep only comes from what others do. A **"Build on this" lineage** is the Stitch analogue: a `posts.built_on` column, an automatic "built on @name's post" chip and a builds count on the parent, with a per-post opt-out. A **video or post reply to a comment** reuses that lineage with the comment quoted. TikTok's **"Add Yours"** prompt sticker, later copied by Instagram and by YouTube Shorts in September 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/11/youtube-shorts-collaborative-add-yours-sticker-is-now-available-to-all-users/)), becomes a lightweight weekly prompt that feeds TNL's tournament engine. The earlier Instagram report reached the same weekly-prompt idea from Dribbble's Warm-up. When TikTok and Dribbble arrive at the same mechanic, it is a strong signal.

Remix credit must not become a rep faucet. TNL's rep sets the seller's commission, so two accounts that keep attaching each other's tracks are farming fee cuts. Every new reuse award should therefore be once per (user, track) for life, subject to the giver weighting and daily caps in the earlier report's P0-1 and P1-1/P1-2. It should also never count a member using their own track. A remix is credit, not a collab: collabs still count only once the other person accepts.

TikTok's music business adds two cheap ideas. "Add to Music App" puts an "Add Song" button beside the track name. **"No money is changing hands"**, and the pick becomes the default service ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/news/tiktok-add-to-music-app)). TNL's version is an optional external link on a track (Spotify, Apple Music or Bandcamp URL), with no integration. TikTok also shut its own streaming app, TikTok Music, on 28 November 2024 "to focus on driving listening on streaming services" ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/09/24/tiktok-music-shutdown/)). That argues against TNL ever trying to be a streaming service. Its strength is the moment of discovery. There it can link out, or sell directly through Stripe Connect.

## TikTok's feed rules transfer as arithmetic, not machine learning

TikTok's public explanation of For You has been stable since June 2020. It ranks on interactions (likes, shares, follows, comments, content created) and on video information (captions, sounds, hashtags). Device and account settings get "lower weight". **"Neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors."** The rules that matter most to TNL are its hard diversity rules. A viewer "generally won't see two videos in a row made with the same sound or by the same creator". Duplicates and already-seen videos are excluded. Videos that "may not appear to be relevant to your expressed interests" are deliberately mixed in to fight the filter bubble ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)). A 2023 update added "dispersal": themes that are fine once but harmful in a run, such as extreme dieting, are swapped out when they cluster ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-ie/safeguarding-recommendations-update)).

The leaked internals show what the public account leaves out. The 2021 "TikTok Algo 101" document, which TikTok confirmed is authentic, scores videos as a weighted sum of predicted actions, **Plike×Vlike + Pcomment×Vcomment + Eplaytime×Vplaytime + Pplay×Vplay**. Its stated goals are retention and time spent. It **penalises categories the user already watched earlier that day** and penalises videos that ask for likes ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/what-makes-tiktok-tick)). The WSJ's 2021 bot study found TikTok learned most hidden interests in **under two hours** from watch time, rewatches and pauses alone. One "sadness" bot reached a feed that was 93% depressive content after 224 videos ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-wall-street-journal-guillaume-chaslot/)). Forbes reported in 2023 that staff "heating" (manually boosting videos) accounted for about **1–2% of daily views**. TikTok put the figure for heated US For You videos at 0.002% ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/01/23/tiktok-admits-its-own-employees-can-decide-what-videos-go-viral/)).

Independent audits put the personalised share in a range. A WWW '24 study of 347 donated real histories found TikTok exploits known interests in **30–50% of the first 1,000 videos**, and that this settles after about 100 videos. Follows and likes were the strongest correlates ([arXiv 2403.12410](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12410v1)). A 2025 replication found watch time *more* important than following. It warned that audit findings go stale quickly and that users drift into niches "just by watching some videos longer" ([arXiv 2504.18140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18140)). The sources do not settle which signal dominates. They agree that consumption signals carry heavy weight, and that a pure watch-time optimiser narrows feeds fast.

ByteDance's real engineering edge is model freshness, not a secret formula. Monolith, its published real-time recommender, joins every action to the features logged when the item was shown, keyed by request ID. It pushes sparse model updates to serving **about every minute**. It lets stale IDs expire and ignores IDs seen too rarely. In a live A/B test, online training beat batch training by 14.0–18.1% in reported relative AUC ([arXiv 2209.07663](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.07663v2)). None of the machine learning scales down to TNL. Three ideas do: **join every response to an impression** (a denominator), **let interest weights decay**, and **don't personalise from one event**.

### How this compares with Instagram, and what TNL should change

TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts have converged on the same structure. Each shows new work to a small seed audience first, sums weighted predicted actions, and runs a final diversity pass. YouTube's Shorts lead described a seed audience and a "viewed vs. swiped away" metric in 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)). The earlier report already proposes most of what this implies for TNL: the impression beacon, rates smoothed per reach toward the lab median, a test audience replacing `newCreatorLift`, "Not interested" and editable lab affinity. **TikTok reinforces all of it.** It adds four things Instagram's material did not:

- **A hard sound rule.** No two adjacent cards share an `audio_track_id`, alongside the existing author damping (`repeatDamp` 0.55).
- **A per-day lab fatigue damp.** For example, multiply by `0.85^n`, where *n* is how many of the viewer's cards today came from that lab.
- **A listen-through label per impression.** Count a complete at 90% of a track, or 60 seconds for long tracks. Count a skip only weakly, since the WWW '24 audit found early skips were not a significant personalisation signal.
- **A fixed exploration share.** Reserve about two in ten slots for labs outside the viewer's top three, with more for new viewers.

TNL's `followBoost` (1.3) fits TikTok's stance. Following personalises *your* feed; it never lifts a creator globally. Every constant above is a starting point to tune. No TikTok source publishes weights, pool sizes or half-lives.

TikTok's history also argues for one thing *not* to copy: the target. Algo 101 optimises retention and time spent. For a community of creatives, the earlier report's measures fit better: the share of new works that reach their test audience within 24 hours, median listen-through, 7-day return rate, and how evenly responders are spread across creators. Any future "staff pick" boost should be logged and capped, and arguably labelled, because heating shows how quietly a manual lever gets abused.

## The design lesson is focus, while regulators target the bottomless feed

Eugene Wei's 2020 analysis explains why TikTok's interface works. The For You feed "is not a scrolling feed. It's paginated, effectively." With one video on screen, **"Everything you do from the moment the video begins playing is signal"**, so the paging friction buys a cleaner signal. "Some forms of friction are good" ([Eugene Wei](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2020/9/18/seeing-like-an-algorithm)). TNL has two places where judging one piece at a time is the point: listening in the Music lab and voting in events. Events already hide vote counts while a stage is open. A full-screen "one by one" view, where each entry gets its own moment and a single Pick button, matches that rule better than a grid that invites comparison.

The same mechanics are now regulatory evidence. On 6 February 2026 the European Commission preliminarily found that TikTok's **"infinite scroll", "autoplay", "push notifications"** and highly personalised recommender "fuel the urge to keep scrolling and shift the brain of users into 'autopilot mode'". It found that TikTok's screen-time tools "do not seem to be effective" and that TikTok "needs to change the basic design of its service" ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-addictive-design-breach-digital-services-act)). TikTok called the findings "categorically false". The maximum fine is 6% of global turnover ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/02/06/tiktoks-addictive-design-breaches-eu-law-commission-says)). The Commission has since opened a similar case against Meta ([EPRS](https://epthinktank.eu/2026/05/06/addictive-design-on-online-platforms/)). In the US, 14 attorneys general sued in October 2024, alleging among other things that users cannot switch autoplay off ([California AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-attorney-general-james-lead-coalition-suing-tiktok)). A 2025 ICWSM study also found that "Not Interested", TikTok's most effective explicit signal, is hard to find in the interface ([ICWSM 2025](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42688)).

TNL starts from a structurally safer place. `rankShowroom` returns a bounded page of 60 (`src/server-10-rank.js:65`), not an endless stream. The rule is to keep it that way. A focus view should run over a finite list: this round's entries, today's new tracks, or the queue. It should end on "You're all caught up". "Show less like this" belongs one tap away on every card, not buried. Re-engagement pushes and streaks should not exist.

The web adds a constraint that doubles as an ethical feature. **Browsers block autoplay with sound until the user interacts with the page**, and a blocked `play()` rejects with `NotAllowedError` ([Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)). A TNL focus view must therefore open from a tap, such as "Start listening" or "Vote one by one". That tap unlocks audio for the session and is also a moment of consent.

The build is plain CSS and JavaScript, with no dependencies. A fixed overlay uses `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory`, and each `100dvh` section uses `scroll-snap-stop: always`, so a fast fling cannot skip entries ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/scroll-snap-stop)). An IntersectionObserver plays the active item and pauses the rest. The client preloads the next item and its poster. The Showroom uses transform and opacity only, which keeps it within the glitch-watcher rule. One event-specific rule matters: **a swipe past an entry must never count as a negative vote**, and entry order should be shuffled on the server for each voter. Otherwise position, not quality, decides close rounds.

TikTok's accessibility record offers features to copy as well. It launched auto captions in April 2021 ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-auto-captions)), and later added photosensitivity warnings for effects that could trigger epilepsy, which viewers can skip ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/auto-captions-on-tiktok)). For TNL that means a creator-typed WebVTT caption track on video posts, and a "contains flashing lights" checkbox that covers the piece until tapped. TikTok Sans (2023) was designed for "legibility and reading retention" over moving video ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-sans)). TNL should take the treatment, not the face: generous line height, and a scrim from palette tokens under captions on media. TikTok's cyan and red glitch logo is TikTok's alone. TNL's equivalent single flourish is already the green accent and the `//` lab marker.

## TikTok's copyable engineering is perceived speed and rollout discipline

ByteDance's stack is the opposite of TNL's in almost every dimension, which makes the transferable part easy to isolate. Go has been its main business language since 2014. Its microservice count grew from roughly **7,000–8,000 in 2018 to over 50,000 by May 2021**, and it had to build its own RPC framework (Kitex), networking layer (Netpoll) and governance just to run them ([CloudWeGo](https://www.cloudwego.io/blog/2022/09/30/kitex-unifying-open-source-practice-for-a-high-performance-rpc-framework/)). Its graph database serves "tens of billions of vertices and trillions of edges" ([PVLDB](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol15/p3306-li.pdf)). Instagram kept one Django monolith and hardened it. ByteDance went microservices first and then built the infrastructure to survive that choice. **For TNL, ByteDance's history confirms the single process; it is not a roadmap.**

The part that transfers is client latency. ByteDance's player SDK attributes TikTok's fast first frame to "pre-loading and pre-rendering strategies" ([BytePlus](https://docs.byteplus.com/byteplus-vod/docs/player-sdk-overview)). A Peking University study of real TikTok traces found adaptive preloading cut stalls by 81% compared with no preloading ([PKU](https://www.icst.pku.edu.cn/NetVideo/docs/20220826143529921247.pdf)). Lynx, ByteDance's UI engine (open-sourced March 2025), is built on "Instant First-Frame Rendering", which never shows a blank screen, and keeps user code off the main thread ([Lynx](https://lynxjs.org/blog/lynx-unlock-native-for-more.html)). tiktok.com's own HTML, inspected on 8 October 2026, is server-rendered React 18. Per-route chunks load from US-specific CDN domains, and a privacy loader and a monitoring SDK load before any product code ([tiktok.com](https://www.tiktok.com/explore)).

The second transferable part is experimentation as discipline. ByteDance's DataTester splits traffic into 100 hash buckets per layer. After a winning variant launches, it holds part of the traffic back on the old version to measure long-term effects ([BytePlus](https://docs.byteplus.com/docs/DataTester/Backtest-Experiment)). TNL cannot power significance tests. It can use the same mechanics for safe rollout and kill switches.

| Practice | TikTok precedent | TNL mapping | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Never paint a blank first frame | Lynx IFR | Inline skeleton or last-seen feed snapshot in `app-01-head.html`/`app-06-body.html` before boot; `skel()` already exists | [no new dep] |
| Prefetch the next one or two media items | Player SDK preloading; PKU study | Range-request the head bytes and poster of the next item in focus view and the track queue (`app-13-tracks-player.js`) | [no new dep] |

TikTok's US situation adds a small hygiene lesson. The US joint venture that closed on 22 January 2026 is to retrain the algorithm "exclusively on U.S. user data" in Oracle's cloud, with Oracle reviewing source code ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/23/jv_announces_tiktok_us_acquisition/)). In May 2026 Senator Markey complained that little has been disclosed about how this works, and asked whether code review could catch malicious code "hidden within an urgent security patch" ([Markey letter](https://www.markey.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_oracle_on_tiktok_usds.pdf)). TNL's version of provenance is cheap: the deploy probe already checks the commit SHA, and a digest of the assembled build closes the gap between "this commit" and "these bytes".

## TikTok's money changes keep proving TNL's fee ladder right

TikTok's business is enormous and increasingly not about ads. ByteDance reported about **$155B revenue in 2024** ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/tiktoks-global-growth-fuels-bytedance-revenue-despite-a-looming-u-s-ban/)), a reported ~$200B in 2025, and ~$120B in H1 2026, with growth credited to international ads, livestreaming and commerce ([Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/bytedance-revenue-hits-usd-120b-ai-spending-cuts-profit)). All of these figures are leaks; none is audited. WARC put TikTok's 2025 global ad revenue at $32.4B ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2025/03/05/forecast-tiktok-ad-revenue-to-top-32bn-in-2025/)). TikTok and Douyin together took **$6B from in-app purchases in 2024**, more than double any other app ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/02/25/tiktok-douyin-in-app-purchases-top-6-billion-annually/)).

The creator side is a record of programmes that shifted under creators' feet. The Creator Fund (2020) split a fixed pool, so each creator's share shrank as more joined. Hank Green reported earning **2.5¢ per 1,000 views** ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2023/11/07/tiktok-shutting-down-creator-fund-creativity-program/)). It gave way to the Creativity Program Beta (2023), then the Creator Rewards Program (2024). That programme pays only for videos over a minute, using an undisclosed formula ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90979212/tiktok-billion-dollar-creator-fund-ending)). Third-party estimates put it at $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/)).

LIVE gifting hides its take inside a currency. The commonly reported conversion leaves TikTok about 50% nominally. Measured against what the viewer actually paid, one analysis finds the creator receives **about 24% for coins bought on the web and under 20% in the app** ([streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-gifts-coins-diamonds)). Series, TikTok's paywalled collections, let creators keep 100% only "for a limited time" ([Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/tiktoks-new-series-feature)). TikTok Shop's US referral fee rose from 2% + $0.30 to **6% in April 2024 and 8% in July 2024** as launch subsidies were cut ([Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/tiktok-shop-hiking-us-seller-fees)).

TikTok Shop also shows what worked. US gross merchandise value grew from about $9B in 2024 to **$15.1B in 2025** (Momentum Works estimates) ([Momentum Works](https://thelowdown.momentum.asia/new-report-tiktok-shop-u-s-gmv-grew-68-to-reach-us15-1b-in-2025/)). Instagram removed its Shop tab in 2023. What Instagram lacked was **affiliate economics**: creators earn roughly 5–20% for selling other people's products ([Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/tiktok-shop-bigger-cut-from-sellers-34244/)). The long tail is brutal: **more than half of TikTok stores made no sales** ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/tiktok-shop-data-analysis/)).

For TNL this points two ways. Affiliate tagging of another member's listing, already on the Instagram report's list, is now backed by TikTok's evidence too. The share should come out of TNL's fee, never the seller's, and should count only once the tagged member accepts. TNL should also expect most listings to sit idle, and use events and labs to send buyers to them.

The contrast is TNL's best marketing line. TikTok subsidised sellers, then raised fees. TNL's commission goes the other way, **falling from 10% to 2% as other members vouch for a seller** (`FEE_BY_LEVEL` in `src/db.js:782`). A written promise that no level's rate will ever rise turns TikTok's fee history into TNL's pitch. The same logic covers tips, if TNL ever adds them. Real dollars through Stripe Connect, with "the artist receives $X of your $Y" shown before payment, beat coins that hide an 80% take. Receiving tips should be 18+ only and Stripe-verified, and tips should never be attached to a leaderboard of fans ranked by spend.

## The build list, in priority order

The order weighs leverage for the creative loop against effort, and puts the rep-integrity prerequisites from the earlier report first wherever a new rep event depends on them. Part sizes were checked against the 24KB limit. `server-08-trust-library.js` is already 23.3KB, so new track routes need a new part. `app-18-media.js` (17.3KB) and `app-13-tracks-player.js` (17.1KB) have room. `app-12-archive-posts.js` (22.4KB) and `app-18-composer.js` (20.5KB) are tight. ## What TNL should deliberately not copy

**Addictive-design patterns are now named in law enforcement.** Infinite scroll, autoplay that cannot be switched off, re-engagement push notifications and opt-in time limits are the specific features in the Commission's February 2026 preliminary DSA findings ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-addictive-design-breach-digital-services-act)) and in the US states' 2024 complaints ([California AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-attorney-general-james-lead-coalition-suing-tiktok)). TNL should keep its bounded Showroom, make every focus view finite, keep mute and pause visible, and never add streaks or night-time nudges. It should also not optimise for time spent: the WSJ rabbit-hole study shows where that target leads ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-wall-street-journal-guillaume-chaslot/)).

**Virtual currencies, gifting and pay-to-engage are where regulators struck hardest.** The EU forced TikTok to permanently withdraw TikTok Lite's watch-to-earn Rewards in August 2024, the first DSA case closed ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/tiktok-commits-permanently-withdraw-tiktok-lite-rewards-programme-eu-comply-digital-services-act)). Utah's lawsuit alleges that TikTok's own "Project Meramec" found minors hosting LIVE and receiving gifts from adults, with TikTok keeping up to half. These are allegations, and TikTok disputes them ([Utah AG](https://attorneygeneral.utah.gov/utah-dcp-and-ags-office-announce-release-of-previously-redacted-information-tiktok-execs-knew-they-were-profiting-off-the-sexual-exploitation-of-minors)). Coins also hide a take of around 80% of what the viewer paid. TNL should use real currency only, show the creator's share before payment, rank no fans by spend, and never pay points for engagement. That last rule matters doubly, because TNL's rep already converts to fee cuts.

**Shifting creator payout programmes destroy trust.** TikTok replaced its main programme three times, ran a fixed pool that shrank per creator as the network grew, and never disclosed its formulas ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2023/11/07/tiktok-shutting-down-creator-fund-creativity-program/)). TNL should not fund a views-based creator pool, should not promise "100% for a limited time", and should not raise fees once sellers depend on it. The fee ladder works because it is published, falls with standing, and comes only from what others do.

**The rest of the skip list:** LIVE battles, a Stories or Now clone (TikTok Now lasted about nine months, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/27/tiktok-kills-bereal-clone-tiktok-now-after-less-than-a-year)), a standalone photo app (TikTok Notes, closed May 2025, [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/tiktok-notes-is-shutting-down-tiktok-pulls-the-plug-on-its-instagram-competitor/)), generative tools like AI Alive or Symphony, paid boosts in the Spark Ads or Promote style, unlogged staff "heating", and TikTok's cyan and red glitch look.

## Conclusion

TikTok's feed gets the attention, but its durable advantage for a creative network is that every post can be reused with credit, and every reuse is visible. That reframes TNL's position. Its rep rule ("only what others do with your work") is exactly the reward that a reuse economy needs, and TikTok only half-built one: its credit was optional, and apart from paying AR-effect makers through Effect Creator Rewards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/tiktok-updates-effect-creator-rewards-lower-eligibility-requirements-new-payout-model-and-more)), its payouts went to views rather than to being built on. TNL can make being built on the thing that lowers your fees. That is a creator economy tied to influence on other creators rather than to time spent, and it is something neither TikTok nor Instagram offers.

The research also shows where the TikTok analogy stops. The evidence for *why* TikTok won comes from vendors and analysts, not controlled studies. The audits disagree on whether follows or watch time drive personalisation, and no TikTok source publishes the weights, pool sizes or decay rates proposed here. TNL should treat every constant as a first guess and measure the result with its own impression log. It should also accept that a network of hundreds grows through seeded labs, weekly prompts and visible credit far more than through clever ranking. The regulatory turn against infinite feeds makes TNL's small, bounded design an asset rather than a limitation, as long as TNL resists the temptation to make it bottomless.
