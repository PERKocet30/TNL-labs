# Give every track a waveform people talk on

*Research synthesis, 8 October 2026. It draws on two research notes in `research_notes/SoundCloud lessons for TNL Labs/` and builds on the TikTok and Instagram reports in `reports/`. Repo facts come from reading the code on branch `research/tiktok-lessons`. SoundCloud facts carry links. TNL facts carry file paths.*

The most useful thing TNL can take from SoundCloud is its oldest feature: **a waveform that listeners comment on at specific moments.** SoundCloud's founders built it as a private feedback tool between collaborators, and it grew into what The Ringer called "probably the only collective listening experience that scales" ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2017/01/04/tech/soundcloud-comments-are-the-only-good-comments-4bc787ee2092)). TNL calls itself a place for "tracks, feedback and chat", yet its Music lab has no waveform, no comments on tracks, no likes on tracks, and a play counter that measures button presses rather than listening. **`POST /api/tracks/:id/play` adds one to `tracks.plays` on every call, rate-limited only at 300 an hour per user. It does not deduplicate, does not require any listening, and counts the owner's own plays** (`src/server-08-trust-library.js:393–398`). The client calls it whenever the player starts a track, unless the start is silent autoplay (`src/app-13-player.js:157`). SoundCloud's 2023–26 discovery work points the same way as the earlier Instagram report. First Fans gives each new upload a small, taste-matched test audience, and a "Liked by your crew" row needs only likes and follows. Both rest on per-listener play data that TNL does not record. All of the audio work can be done with the `ffmpeg-static` binary TNL already ships: real waveform peaks, server-measured duration, loudness analysis and one consistent streaming rendition. SoundCloud's engineering history argues firmly against splitting TNL's single server. Its business turn, to zero commission on fan payments and direct sales, puts mild pressure on TNL's 10%-to-2% fee ladder but does not overturn it. The build order follows from that. First, make a play mean a listen. Then add waveforms, timed comments and track likes. Then add a free "first ears" slot, and only after that any feature that ranks or rewards on plays.

## The waveform began as a feedback tool, and TNL's player has none

SoundCloud's founders, a sound designer and a musician, replaced the iTunes-style progress bar with a waveform. Listeners could pin a comment to a moment, and Alexander Ljung described the audio as a "social object, almost like a campfire that people gather around" ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2017/01/04/tech/soundcloud-comments-are-the-only-good-comments-4bc787ee2092)). The format explains why it worked. **Comments cluster on drops, choruses and intros. Each one flashes and fades as playback passes it. The thread ends when the song ends.** Short reactions are easy to take in, and long or spammy ones are easy to ignore. The cost was spam: follower begging, bots and trolling, the same source reports. SoundCloud still invests here. It added comment likes in March 2024, and a "Comments hub" sits in its Artist Pro tier ([Newsroom](https://soundcloud.com/company/newsroom); [pricing](https://soundcloud.com/getstarted/pricing)). No source gives engagement data for timed comments, so their value rests on longevity and cultural evidence rather than measurement.

TNL's track model has none of this. The `tracks` table holds `id, user_id, title, url, artwork_url, description, duration_ms, bytes, plays, created_at`. It has no likes, comments, waveform, privacy or download columns (`src/db.js:306–317`). The only waveform in the app decorates voice notes, and it is "seeded from the file name" rather than drawn from the audio (`src/app-10-chat-1-kit.js:90`). SoundCloud computes each track's waveform once and serves it as a small JSON of `samples[]` that clients draw ([freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-make-realtime-soundcloud-waveforms-in-react-native-4df0f4c6b3cc/)). The BBC's peaks.js documentation explains why: decoding audio in the browser is CPU-heavy and means downloading the whole file first ([bbc/peaks.js](https://github.com/bbc/peaks.js)). The TNL version is one ffmpeg pass on upload. Decode to mono 8 kHz PCM, fold the samples into about 200 max-amplitude buckets, and store them as `tracks.peaks`, about 600 bytes of JSON. The same pass yields the true duration, which closes a trust gap. Today `duration_ms` comes from the client, because "ffprobe is a separate package" (`src/server-08-trust-library.js`, extract route).

Timed comments follow naturally. They need a `track_comments(track_id, user_id, at_ms, body ≤ 140)` table, avatar dots on the waveform, and a comment that fades in for about three seconds as the playhead passes it, animated on opacity only so the glitch watcher stays quiet. The composer pre-fills "comment at 1:42". **Writing a comment should earn no rep.** TNL's rule is that rep comes only from what others do with your work. A comment is feedback the owner receives, and paying for it would invite the follower-begging spam that SoundCloud is known for. This deepens the TikTok report's track page `/t/:id` rather than competing with it. TikTok's lesson was a "Built with this" grid of derivatives. SoundCloud's lesson is that the centre of the page should be the track itself, with its listeners' reactions laid along it.

Reposts are the cautionary half of SoundCloud's social design. A repost pushes a track into every follower's stream ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/?p=8024)). Fortune reported in 2017 that a design flaw in reposting "opened the floodgates for promoters to manipulate listeners' feeds", and that SoundCloud did not try to fix it until 2015 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2017/07/23/soundcloud-music-streaming-troubles)). Repost chains and paid repost services grew from that gap. This **reinforces the Instagram report's P0-2 fix**: rep for a share is awarded once per (actor, original), and the share route gets a rate limit. A TNL track reshare should reuse `share_received` (+3) under that rule. A reshare should not be resharable, and reshares should be capped at about one in five items of a follower's feed.

## First Fans is the test audience the Instagram report proposed, at 100 listeners

SoundCloud's discovery since 2023 runs on three ideas, and TNL can adopt all three without paid reach. **First Fans** recommends each new upload to up to 100 listeners chosen from their past listening, and widens to up to 1,000 for the best performers. SoundCloud framed it against "45.6 million tracks across services" that got zero plays the previous year, and claims a roughly 400% average lift in listens. That is a company figure with no independent check ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-launches-fan-powered-buzzing-playlists-to-spotlight-up-and-coming-artists)). **Buzzing Playlists** (May 2024) gather the First Fans tracks that drew the most likes, comments, reposts, replays and playlist adds into weekly per-genre lists ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/music/soundcloud-buzzing-playlists-new-feature/)). The **2025 social homepage** adds "Liked by your crew", a daily list of tracks recently liked by friends and favourite artists, and a "Trending Trackwall" that can be filtered by local scene or community ([RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/soundclouds-new-social-discovery-features/)).

First Fans **reinforces the Instagram report's guaranteed test audience** (about 30 impressions within 48 hours, promoted only if the smoothed rate beats the lab median). It differs in two ways that matter for music. First, SoundCloud judges by listening behaviour (replays, playlist adds), not only taps, so TNL's version needs the listen log described in the next section before it can judge anything. Second, SoundCloud sells First Fans as part of a paid plan, which TNL's no-paid-reach rule forbids. TNL's "first ears" slot should be free and universal. Show each new track, for 48 hours, to a capped number of members who have listened in its channel, at most one such slot per member per day. Widen it if completion and likes beat the channel median. The ranking code has room for this (`src/server-10-rank.js`, 7.7KB), although it does not touch tracks today.

"Liked by people you follow" is the cheapest item on the list once track likes exist: one SQL join over likes and follows, shown as a row at the top of `// Music`. SoundCloud's "scene" filter corresponds to TNL's channels and tags, not to geography. Its February 2026 report is framed around "where music scenes form before they're named" ([Newsroom](https://soundcloud.com/company/newsroom)), and TNL's labs are scenes by design.

## A play must mean a listen before anything ranks or rewards on it

The counter is the foundation, and today it is the weakest part. As noted above, every press of play adds one, and the owner's own plays count. SoundCloud does not publish its play-counting rule, so the specific thresholds below are TNL design choices, not SoundCloud facts. Its royalty design does show the principle. **Fan-Powered Royalties (April 2021) splits each listener's subscription or ad revenue among the artists that listener actually played.** Payouts depend on each fan's listening time as a share of that fan's own monthly total ([SoundCloud Help](https://help.soundcloud.com/hc/articles/1260801306810); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2021/03/02/soundcloud-fan-powered-royalties/)). Fraud therefore limits itself, because a bot's plays only move that bot account's own share. FPR's payout results are disputed. SoundCloud cites "up to 500%" increases for some artists ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/soundcloud-says-some-artists-have-seen-500-increase-royalty-payments-its-new-plan)), and a MIDiA study is reported to find that just over half of artists earn more than under the pooled model ([Chartlex](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/streaming/is-soundcloud-worth-it-for-artists-2026), secondary). The design principle stands regardless: **weight comes from the listener's own budget, so one account cannot inflate another.** That is exactly the shape of TNL's rule that rep comes only from others, expressed as per-actor caps.

The fix extends the TikTok report's listen-through log rather than adding a second system. The client stops calling `api.trackPlay` on press. Instead it sends `POST /api/tracks/:id/listen {ms, max_pos_ms}` on pause, end, track change and `pagehide` (via `sendBeacon`). The server upserts a `track_listens(track_id, user_id, day, ms, max_pos)` row. `plays` rises only when the listener is not the owner, has listened for at least 30 seconds or half the track, and has not already counted that day. The existing 300-an-hour limit stays as a backstop. **Plays still earn no rep**, which keeps them unfarmable. The same table unlocks owner-only insights that SoundCloud keeps behind Artist Pro: plays, unique listeners, average completion, a drop-off curve and where plays came from ([Help: advanced Insights](https://help.soundcloud.com/hc/en-us/articles/45787456969115-Your-advanced-Insights)). It also unlocks **Fan Recognition**, SoundCloud's February 2025 module listing a track's "First Fans" (top listeners in week one) and "Top Fans" (all time). It shows only listeners who liked the track and follow the artist, and fans can opt out ([SoundCloud press](https://press.soundcloud.com/247205-introducing-fan-recognition-celebrate-the-fans-who-fuel-your-music)). It gives the listener status without giving the artist rep, which suits TNL's rules.

Likes are the other signal TNL's ranking would need. A heart on track rows and in the player bar should reuse `like_received` (+6) for the owner, once per liker per track, through `awardRep`. That should wait for the Instagram report's P0-1 (`actor_id` on `rep_events`) and P1-1 daily caps, so the new rep faucet opens with the guards already in place. One correction to an easy assumption: the +4 `sound_used` award applies only to Studio library samples. Putting another member's *track* on a post pays its owner nothing today. SoundCloud's reposts and comments confirm that the TikTok report's "Use this sound" credit (item 2 there) is worth building. SoundCloud adds nothing that changes its design.

## TNL's ffmpeg can do SoundCloud's audio pipeline at TNL's scale

SoundCloud has spent 2025–26 cutting its formats down. Its public API retired progressive MP3, HLS MP3 and HLS Opus on 31 December 2025. It now serves **AAC over HLS at 160 kbps (preferred) and 96 kbps (fallback)**, for "more consistent playback, and better support across devices" ([Backstage, Sep 2025](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/api-streaming-urls)). In June 2026 it switched its encoder to Fraunhofer libfdk_aac, its first encoder change in over a decade. It also explained why it rolls off audio around 17 kHz at 256 kbps: the filter lowers error in the mid bands, and it was backed by a blind listening test ([Backstage, Jun 2026](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/less-is-more-why-soundcloud-low-passes-its-aac-transcodings)). Its upload guidance asks for lossless masters with about 0.5–1 dB of headroom, so transcoding does not clip ([Help: Uploading Requirements](https://help.soundcloud.com/hc/en-us/articles/46020588217499-Uploading-Requirements)).

TNL stores uploaded tracks exactly as uploaded. Only audio extracted from video is encoded, to AAC 128k with `+faststart`, behind a one-job `EXTRACTING` boolean lock with a 120-second timeout (`src/server-08-trust-library.js:282–330`). Uploads are served by `express.static` with hashed, immutable, year-long cache headers (`src/server-01-boot.js:188–199`), and `express.static` handles HTTP Range requests by default, so seeking already works. **HLS is not worth building at TNL's scale.** Progressive files with Range requests and a cache in front cover seeking, and HLS's adaptive bitrate matters at SoundCloud's 400-million-track scale ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/as-soundcloud-overhauls-its-creator-subscription-model-ceo-eliah-seton-says-the-platform-is-building-musics-next-major-revenue-format1/)), not TNL's.

What TNL should copy is the single rendition. Transcode each upload once to AAC-LC at 160k with `+faststart`, using ffmpeg's native `aac` encoder. libfdk_aac is not in `ffmpeg-static` because of licensing, though nobody has yet checked the encoder list with `ffmpeg -encoders`. Consider `-cutoff 17000`, following SoundCloud's finding. Keep the original for downloads and market sales. In the same pass, measure integrated loudness (`loudnorm=print_format=json`) and store the LUFS figure, then apply per-track gain on the client so a quiet demo and a loud master sit together in the queue. Whether SoundCloud itself normalises loudness is undocumented. Every extra ffmpeg pass costs CPU on the same container that serves requests. That makes **the Instagram report's P1-6 `jobs` table the gate for all audio work.** Replace the boolean lock with a SQLite queue drained by one in-process worker, so peaks, loudness and transcodes survive restarts and never block a request. Measure how long a ten-minute track takes on Railway's CPU before switching transcoding on for every upload.

## SoundCloud's microservices history argues for keeping one server

SoundCloud began as one Rails monolith, "the mothership". From 2012 it extracted services, first in Clojure and JRuby, then in Scala and Finagle ([Calçado, 2015](https://philcalcado.com/2015/09/08/how_we_ended_up_with_microservices.html)). The driver was organisational, not technical. Value-stream mapping found that **of 47 days a feature spent in engineering, only 11 were actual work**, and the rest was queueing between separate web and back-end teams. The documented lessons are narrow and pointed. Phil Calçado warned that growing organisations must watch "the fixed cost of each service", and that "what's everybody's responsibility is nobody's responsibility". SoundCloud built a Scala proxy in 2014 to strangle its Rails public API, then left it "somewhat loosely" finished for six years. That left duplicated code paths and security exposure from deprecated Rails versions. The code was finally deleted in 2022, with the lesson "make sure to have a plan to complete the migration before the knowledge is lost" ([Backstage, Mar 2022](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/end-of-the-strangler)). To see its services at all, SoundCloud needed its own PaaS ("Bazooka"), then Kubernetes trials, then a new monitoring system, Prometheus, started in 2012 and donated to the CNCF in 2016 ([History of Prometheus](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/the-history-of-prometheus-at-soundcloud/65464158); [The Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/cloud/2016/05/10/google-and-friends-add-prometheus-to-kubernetes-platform/1638818)). In July 2017 it cut 173 of about 420 staff, after warning it could run out of cash ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/07/06/soundcloud-cuts-40-percent-of-staff.html)). No source ties the layoffs to the architecture. Still, a much smaller company inherited an estate built for a larger one, and the 2022 strangler post reads partly as paying down that debt.

TNL has one deployable and a handful of contributors, so it should take the benefits without the costs. **Backends-for-Frontends**, which SoundCloud pioneered around 2013, existed because a profile screen needed several chatty calls to a shared public API, and changing shared endpoints blocked A/B tests ([Calçado, BFF](https://philcalcado.com/2015/09/18/the_back_end_for_front_end_pattern_bff.html)). TNL gets the same benefit from one endpoint per screen. For example, `GET /api/music/home` could return tracks, peaks, the "liked by" row and first-ears slots in one response. Calçado's follow-on lesson applies too: when two BFFs duplicated the same profile shaping, it was "a bad smell indicating that we were missing an object in our domain model". In a monolith the fix is a shared function, like the existing `shapeTrack`, not a service. **Prometheus-style metrics** cost nothing as an admin- or token-gated plain-text `/metrics` route. Useful counters are jobs queued, ffmpeg failures, plays counted versus rejected, and per-route p99. Scraping them with Grafana Cloud or Prometheus would be a new service, which needs the owner's approval. SoundCloud's August 2026 post adds a hot-path rule. One guard that skipped a warning log and four metric increments cut its ad service's p99 from about 48 ms to about 18 ms ([Backstage, Aug 2026](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/one-line-of-python-and-a-3x-drop-in-p99)). TNL's listen beacon will be its busiest write, so it should not log per request. Split a worker out only if ffmpeg measurably hurts request latency, and even then as a second Railway process reading the same `jobs` table.

TNL's front end already matches SoundCloud's 2012 "Next" design, which was a single-page app so that "users [can] start listening to a sound and continue exploring without ever breaking the experience", built from concatenated module packages ([Backstage, 2012](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/building-the-next-soundcloud)). TNL concatenates numbered parts and keeps one module-level `Audio` singleton with a queue outside the re-rendered DOM (`src/app-13-player.js`). The gap is in the data, not the architecture.

## SoundCloud now charges creators by subscription and takes nothing on fan money

SoundCloud reported its first EBITDA-positive year in 2023: guidance of €288m revenue and €2m EBITDA, against −€29m in 2022, with a 36% gross margin ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2023/12/14/soundcloud-says-it-will-be-profitable-in-latest-financial-year/)). Its owners explored a sale at a reported valuation above $1B in January 2024 ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/soundcloud-reportedly-going-on-sale-for-over-1billion-news)), and no sale has been reported since. Since late 2025 its strategy has been to **charge creators a subscription and take no commission on fan money**. Artist ($39/yr) and Artist Pro ($99/yr) bundle promotion, stats, scheduling and distribution ([pricing](https://soundcloud.com/getstarted/pricing)). Distribution's 20% revenue share was dropped from the end-of-November 2025 payout cycle, and a profile Support button carries no SoundCloud commission ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/soundcloud-launches-all-one-artist-subscription-plan-including-100-distribution-royalties)). In August 2026 about 200 US Artist Pro creators began selling MP3 or WAV downloads from their profiles at zero commission, after SoundCloud found it was sending more than 500,000 users a year off-platform to buy music ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-starts-letting-artists-sell-downloads-direct-from-their-profiles-and-says-its-taking-zero-commission/)). CEO Eliah Seton's summary: "Streaming is not enough for artists" ([MBW, Nov 2025](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/as-soundcloud-overhauls-its-creator-subscription-model-ceo-eliah-seton-says-the-platform-is-building-musics-next-major-revenue-format1/)).

This **differs from the TikTok report's comfortable verdict on TNL's fees.** TikTok's fees rose, so TNL's falling ladder looked good by contrast. SoundCloud's fees fell to zero. TNL's ladder (`FEE_BY_LEVEL = {1:10, 2:8, 3:6, 4:4, 5:2}`, `src/db.js:782`) still wins for small sellers. At 10%, a level-1 seller pays less than Artist Pro's $99 a year until sales pass $990. At level 5's 2%, the break-even is $4,950. TNL needs no subscription, and SoundCloud's zero-commission sales are a US beta for a few hundred creators. The pitch should therefore say so plainly: no subscription, and a fee that only falls. TNL should also add a "Buy / download" link from a track to its existing Market sound listing, which already has `audio_url`, `bpm`, `musical_key`, `stems` and a free filter (`src/server-06-settings-payouts-market.js:157–262`). That puts the sale next to discovery without a second payment path. Before paid downloads grow, note that **`src/pay.js` has no Stripe webhook**. Checkout sessions are created and later checked by polling, so a buyer who closes the tab depends on the next poll to get the file. Adding a signed webhook is the payment work SoundCloud's direct sales imply, and it needs `/security-review`.

Two further SoundCloud episodes bear on TNL's rules. In March 2026 it launched **Follower Exclusive Releases**, which let artists release to followers before the public ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-launches-superfan-feature-that-lets-artists-release-music-exclusively-to-followers-before-wider-release/)), alongside resettable secret links for private tracks ([Help](https://help.soundcloud.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003449467-Resetting-a-secret-link)). TNL can offer both free, with a separate resettable `share_token` column, because lab and track IDs never change. Second, the **2025 AI-terms backlash**. A February 2024 clause allowing uploads to be used for AI surfaced in May 2025. Within days the CEO conceded the wording "was too broad and wasn't clear enough" and promised that any generative training would be opt-in ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-fixes-ai-policy-following-backlash-ceo-eliah-seton-says-ai-should-support-artists-not-replace-them)). For TNL, the terms are part of the product. The Studio sound library and "Use this sound" need a one-line statement of what TNL may do with uploads before they grow. SoundCloud's culture of bedroom producers, SoundCloud rap and DJ mixes grew from low-friction posting and immediate feedback. It also brought years of copyright friction, including automated takedowns until 2016 licensing deals let DJ mixes stay up ([The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2016/12/12/soundcloud-no-longer-remove-dj-mixes-for-copyright-infringement)). TNL should keep reuse inside member-owned content with the owner's consent, as the extract route already does.

A read-only count of `tracks` and the `plays` distribution on the live database would show whether the first-ears cap should be measured in tens or in single digits.

## What TNL should deliberately not copy

**Paid reach in any form.** SoundCloud's "Promote tracks", "Get playlisted" quotas and the paid First Fans access in its plans ([pricing](https://soundcloud.com/getstarted/pricing)) break TNL's rule that standing comes only from what others do. So does the repost-trading economy that grew around cheap reposts ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2017/07/23/soundcloud-music-streaming-troubles)). **Locking artist controls behind a subscription** is the opposite of TNL's advantage. SoundCloud charges for scheduling, replace-track, the comments hub and deep stats. TNL can give all of them to every member. **Distribution to streaming services, mastering credits, and AI-generation partner tools** would each bring new services or dependencies. **HLS, multiple codecs and a transcoding service** solve problems TNL does not have. **Microservices, a custom PaaS and in-house monitoring** were costs of SoundCloud's 400-person organisation, and the half-finished strangler shows what a small team inherits when it copies them. **Fan-Powered Royalties** needs a revenue pool TNL does not have, though its principle survives as per-actor caps. Finally, **quiet terms changes**: SoundCloud's 2025 episode shows how fast creators notice, and how much trust a one-line clarification buys back.

## Conclusion

SoundCloud's history shows that what made it matter to creators was cheap and social, not expensive. A precomputed waveform and comments pinned to moments made a track a place to gather. Its costliest efforts, the service sprawl, repost economics and format churn, mostly bought scale or revenue rather than creative value. SoundCloud's own recent work points in TNL's direction. It now gives new work a small test audience, takes no commission on fan money, and promises creators consent over AI use. In each case it got there late, under a paywall or after a backlash. TNL can start where SoundCloud ended up, and give these features to every member for free.

The sharpest new insight is the order of work. Every SoundCloud feature worth copying, from Buzzing and Fan Recognition to insights and First Fans, rests on per-listener listening data. TNL's single number, `plays`, measures button presses. Fixing that counter is unglamorous, but it is the feature that makes the other features possible. The TikTok report's listen log and the Instagram report's impression log turn out to be one investment, and SoundCloud is the strongest case for making it first.
